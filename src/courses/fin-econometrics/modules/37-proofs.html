?●P1 · lecture 2Derive the OLS estimator, with F.O.C. and S.O.C.

Start from $\min_b(y-Xb)'(y-Xb)$.

Solution

$SSE=y'y-2y'Xb+b'X'Xb$. F.O.C. $-2X'y+2X'Xb=0\Rightarrow X'Xb=X'y$. A1b ⇒ $X'X$ invertible ⇒ $b=(X'X)^{-1}X'y$. S.O.C. $2X'X$ positive definite, since $z'X'Xz=\lVert Xz\rVert^2>0$ for $z\neq0$. Minimum.

?●P2 · lecture 3Derive OLS as a projection

Use $e\perp X$. Show also $\hat y'e=0$ and the decomposition of $y'y$.

Solution

$X'e=0\Rightarrow X'(y-Xb)=0\Rightarrow b=(X'X)^{-1}X'y$. $\hat y'e=b'X'e=0$. $y'y=(\hat y+e)'(\hat y+e)=\hat y'\hat y+2\hat y'e+e'e=\hat y'\hat y+e'e$. So $R^2=\hat y'\hat y/y'y=1-e'e/y'y$ (de-meaned) lies in $[0,1]$.

?●●P3 · lecture 3Slope = covariance / variance

One regressor and a constant.

Solution

Normal equations: $\sum e_i=0\Rightarrow b_1=\bar y-b_2\bar x$; $\sum x_ie_i=0\Rightarrow\sum x_iy_i-(\bar y-b_2\bar x)n\bar x-b_2\sum x_i^2=0$. Divide by $n$: $b_2=\frac{\frac1n\sum x_iy_i-\bar x\bar y}{\frac1n\sum x_i^2-\bar x^2}$.

?●P4 · lecture 4Unbiasedness

State the assumptions used.

Solution

$b=Ay$, $A=(X'X)^{-1}X'$. $\mathbb E[b\mid X]=A\,\mathbb E[y\mid X]=AX\beta=\beta$ (A1b, A2a). $\mathbb E[b]=\mathbb E[\mathbb E[b\mid X]]=\beta$.

?●P5 · lecture 4Variance of b

State the assumption used.

Solution

$\mathbb V[b\mid X]=A\mathbb V[y\mid X]A'=A\sigma^2I_nA'=\sigma^2AA'=\sigma^2(X'X)^{-1}X'X(X'X)^{-1}=\sigma^2(X'X)^{-1}$ (A2b).

?●●●P6 · lecture 4Gauss–Markov

Full proof.

Solution

$\tilde b=\tilde Ay$; unbiased ⇔ $\tilde AX=I_K$. $\tilde A=A+D$ ⇒ $DX=0$. $\mathbb V[\tilde b\mid X]=\sigma^2(A+D)(A+D)'=\sigma^2AA'+\sigma^2AD'+\sigma^2DA'+\sigma^2DD'$. $AD'=(X'X)^{-1}(DX)'=0$. So $\mathbb V[\tilde b\mid X]=\mathbb V[b\mid X]+\sigma^2DD'$ and $x'DD'x=\lVert D'x\rVert^2\ge0$.

?●●P7 · lecture 4Properties of M

Symmetric, $MX=0$, idempotent, rank $n-K$; $e=M\varepsilon$.

Solution

$M=I-X(X'X)^{-1}X'$. $M'=I-X[(X'X)^{-1}]'X'=M$. $MX=X-X=0$. $MM=M-MX(X'X)^{-1}X'=M$. rank = trace $=n-\text{tr}((X'X)^{-1}X'X)=n-K$. $e=My=MX\beta+M\varepsilon=M\varepsilon$.

?●●P8 · lecture 4s² is unbiased

Show $\mathbb E[e'e\mid X]=\sigma^2(n-K)$.

Solution

$e'e=\varepsilon'M\varepsilon$. $\mathbb E[\varepsilon'M\varepsilon\mid X]=\mathbb E[\text{tr}(\varepsilon\varepsilon'M)\mid X]=\text{tr}(\mathbb E[\varepsilon\varepsilon'\mid X]M)=\sigma^2\text{tr}(M)=\sigma^2(n-K)$. Hence $\mathbb E[e'e/(n-K)]=\sigma^2$.

?●●P9 · lecture 5Distribution of m under H₀

With normal errors.

Solution

$\varepsilon\mid X\sim N(0,\sigma^2I)\Rightarrow y\mid X\sim N(X\beta,\sigma^2I)\Rightarrow b\mid X\sim N(\beta,\sigma^2(X'X)^{-1})\Rightarrow m=Rb-q\mid X\sim N(R\beta-q,\sigma^2R(X'X)^{-1}R')$, and $R\beta-q=0$ under $H_0$. Quadratic form: $m'(\mathbb V[m\mid X])^{-1}m\sim\chi^2_J$.

?●●●P10 · lecture 7Distribution of the t-statistic

Three ingredients.

Solution

(1) $z=b_1/\sqrt{\sigma^2R(X'X)^{-1}R'}\sim N(0,1)$ under $H_0$. (2) $s^2/\sigma^2=\frac{(\varepsilon/\sigma)'M(\varepsilon/\sigma)}{n-K}\sim\chi^2_{n-K}/(n-K)$. (3) $\text{Cov}[b,e\mid X]=\sigma^2(X'X)^{-1}X'M=0$ and joint normality ⇒ $b$, $s^2$ independent. $t=z/\sqrt{s^2/\sigma^2}$ is the ratio defining $t_{n-K}$.

?●●P11 · lecture 8Distribution of the F-statistic

From the $\chi^2_J$ statistic.

Solution

$F=\frac{m'(\sigma^2R(X'X)^{-1}R')^{-1}m/J}{(e'e/\sigma^2)/(n-K)}=\frac{\chi^2_J/J}{\chi^2_{n-K}/(n-K)}$, independent ⇒ $F_{J,n-K}$.

?●●P12 · time seriesAR(1): Wold form, mean, variance, ACF

Assume $\lvert\phi_1\rvert<1$.

Solution

Back-substitute: $Y_t=\frac{\phi_0}{1-\phi_1}+\sum_j\phi_1^j\varepsilon_{t-j}$. $\mathbb E=\frac{\phi_0}{1-\phi_1}$. $\mathbb V=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum\phi_1^{2j}=\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{1-\phi_1^2}$. With $\phi_0=0$: $\text{Cov}[Y_{t+1},Y_t]=\mathbb E[\mathbb E[(\phi_1Y_t+\varepsilon_{t+1})Y_t\mid Y_t]]=\phi_1\mathbb V[Y_t]$, iterate: $\phi_1^h\mathbb V[Y_t]$, so $\rho(h)=\phi_1^h$.

?●P13 · GARCHUnconditional variance of GARCH(1,1)

And show $\mathbb E[r_t]=0$, $\text{Cov}[r_t,r_{t-1}]=0$.

Solution

$\bar\sigma^2=\beta_0+\beta_1\bar\sigma^2+\beta_2\bar\sigma^2\Rightarrow\bar\sigma^2=\beta_0/(1-\beta_1-\beta_2)$. $\mathbb E[r_t]=\mathbb E[\sigma_t\mathbb E[z_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]]=0$. $\mathbb E[r_tr_{t-1}]=\mathbb E[r_{t-1}\sigma_t\mathbb E[z_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]]=0$.

?●P14 · GARCHVaR and ES in a model $r_{t+1}=\sigma_{t+1}z_{t+1}$

Derive both formulas, and ES for a normal $z$.

Solution

$\alpha=P(\sigma_{t+1}z_{t+1}\lt-VaR\mid\mathcal F_t)=P(z\lt-VaR/\sigma_{t+1})\Rightarrow VaR=-\sigma_{t+1}z_\alpha$. $-ES=\mathbb E[r_{t+1}\mid r_{t+1}\lt-VaR,\mathcal F_t]=\sigma_{t+1}\mathbb E[z\mid z<z_\alpha]$. Normal: $\frac1\alpha\int_{-\infty}^{z_\alpha}x\varphi(x)dx=-\varphi(z_\alpha)/\alpha$.
