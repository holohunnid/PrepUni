The assumptions A1, A2, A3

lecture 1, slides 7–10
READ FIRST Which assumptions are essential, and which are convenience— ⊡ ✕

A model is an equation plus assumptions about (A1) $X$, (A2) $\varepsilon$, (A3) how they relate. The lecture keeps saying which ones "cannot be relaxed" and which "can easily be relaxed". Learn the list that way.

The sentence that earns the marks
$$\mathbb E[\varepsilon\mid X]=0\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbb E[y\mid X]=\mathbb E[X\beta+\varepsilon\mid X]=X\beta+\mathbb E[\varepsilon\mid X]=X\beta$$

"So $\beta$ determines the conditional expectation of $y$ given $X$."

KEY What A2b looks like, entry by entry— ⊡ ✕
$$\mathbb E[\varepsilon\varepsilon'\mid X]=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb E[\varepsilon_1^2]&\mathbb E[\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2]&\cdots\\\mathbb E[\varepsilon_2\varepsilon_1]&\mathbb E[\varepsilon_2^2]&\\\vdots&&\ddots\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma^2&0&\cdots\\0&\sigma^2&\\\vdots&&\ddots\end{bmatrix}$$

Diagonal equal: every error has the same variance (homoskedasticity).

Off-diagonal zero: errors of different individuals are uncorrelated.

Violations you will meet

  • Volatility clustering: variance of returns changes over time (GARCH, module 32). The diagonal is not constant.
  • Autocorrelation: today's error is related to yesterday's (time series). The off-diagonal is not zero.

With another covariance matrix $\sigma^2\Omega$, OLS is still linear and unbiased (that uses only A2a), but $\sigma^2(X'X)^{-1}$ is no longer its variance and it is no longer "best".

KEY Slide question · what is the problem with stringent assumptions?— ⊡ ✕

The more you assume, the less general the model. Stringent assumptions make the maths easy and the conclusions strong, but if they fail in the data the conclusions fail with them. Economists therefore keep the assumptions that give the parameters meaning (A1b, A2a) and treat the rest as convenience.

INTERACTIVE What breaks when an assumption fails— ⊡ ✕

Try this. "Nothing": centred at 1. Endogeneity: the histogram moves away from 1, and raising $n$ makes it more concentrated around the wrong number. More data does not cure endogeneity. Heteroskedasticity: still centred at 1 (unbiased), but the textbook standard error is wrong. Near-collinearity: centred at 1 and the formula is right, but the spread explodes: the data cannot tell $x_2$ from $x_3$.

EXAMPLES How A3 fails in finance— ⊡ ✕
  • Reverse causality. Regress a fund's return on its inflows. Good returns attract inflows, so $y\Rightarrow X$ as well.
  • Omitted variable. Wage on education, with ability in $\varepsilon$: ability drives both.
  • Measurement error. The regressor is observed with noise; the noise ends up in $\varepsilon$ and in $x$.

One omitted variable, quickly. Truth: $y=X\beta+\gamma z+u$, but you leave $z$ out, so $\varepsilon=\gamma z+u$. Then

$$\mathbb E[b\mid X,z]=\beta+(X'X)^{-1}X'z\,\gamma.$$

Bias = (coefficient of $z$) × (what you get regressing $z$ on $X$). No bias if $\gamma=0$ or $X'z=0$.

W3 · EX 3 (from Greene) · errors with a non-zero mean

$y_i=\alpha+\beta x_i+\varepsilon_i$, $x_i$ fixed and not all equal, density $f(\varepsilon_i)=\frac1\lambda e^{-\varepsilon_i/\lambda}$ for $\varepsilon_i\ge0$, so $\mathbb E[\varepsilon_i\mid x_i]=\lambda$ and $\mathbb V[\varepsilon_i\mid x_i]=\lambda^2$. Show that the OLS slope is unbiased and the intercept is biased.

  1. A2a fails: the errors have mean $\lambda>0$, not 0.
  2. Move the mean into the intercept: $y_i=(\alpha+\lambda)+\beta x_i+(\varepsilon_i-\lambda)=\alpha^*+\beta x_i+\varepsilon_i^*$.
  3. The new error is well behaved: $\mathbb E[\varepsilon_i^*\mid x_i]=0$, $\mathbb V[\varepsilon_i^*\mid x_i]=\lambda^2$. All assumptions hold for the starred model.
  4. So OLS is unbiased for $(\alpha^*,\beta)$: $\mathbb E[b_2]=\beta$ ✓ and $\mathbb E[b_1]=\alpha^*=\alpha+\lambda\neq\alpha$.
  5. Conclusion. Slope unbiased; intercept biased by $\lambda$. A constant non-zero mean of the error is absorbed by the intercept. That is one reason a constant term is so useful.
Why "not all equal"?

If all $x_i$ were equal, the column $x$ would be a multiple of the column of ones, A1b would fail, and there would be no unique OLS estimator.

?●●YES / NO with a reasonTrue or false?

(a) OLS needs normal errors to be unbiased. (b) If $\mathbb V[\varepsilon\mid X]=\sigma^2\Omega\neq\sigma^2I$, OLS is biased. (c) A1b can be relaxed if $n$ is large. (d) $\mathbb E[\varepsilon\mid X]=0$ implies $\mathbb E[\varepsilon]=0$.

Solution

(a) False: unbiasedness uses only A1b and A2a. (b) False: the variance matrix plays no role in $\mathbb E[b\mid X]=\beta$. (c) False: perfect collinearity is not cured by more rows. (d) True by the tower property: $\mathbb E[\varepsilon]=\mathbb E[\mathbb E[\varepsilon\mid X]]=0$.

?●●practiceIdentify the violation

(i) Daily returns with calm and turbulent periods. (ii) Regressing house prices on size in m² and size in square feet. (iii) Regressing police numbers on crime to learn the effect of crime on police, where police also reduce crime.

Solution

(i) A2b: the variance is not constant. (ii) A1b: the two columns are proportional, $\text{rank}(X)\lt K$. (iii) A3: $y$ affects $X$.
