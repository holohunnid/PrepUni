OLS as a projection

lecture 3, slides 7–11
READ FIRST Forget the scatter plot: think of y as one arrow— ⊡ ✕

In the scatter plot each observation is a point. In the geometric view, the whole vector $y$ is one arrow in $\mathbb R^n$, and each column of $X$ is another arrow. The columns of $X$ generate a flat subspace (a "floor"). $y$ sticks out of the floor. OLS drops $y$ straight down: the shadow is $\hat y$, and the vertical stick is $e$.

DEF The column space and the projection— ⊡ ✕

The $K$ linearly independent columns of $X=[X_1\cdots X_K]$ generate (span) a vector space: any $Z$ in it is

$$Z=X_1b_1+\dots+X_Kb_K=Xb .$$

In general $y$ is not in that space. We project it:

$$\hat y=X_1b_1+\dots+X_Kb_K=Xb,\qquad e=y-\hat y,\qquad y=\hat y+e$$

A projection can be defined in two equivalent ways:

$$\text{1) }\min_b\lVert e\rVert^2\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\text{2) }e\perp X$$
  1. Definition 1: shortest stick. $\min\lVert e\rVert^2=\min e'e=\min SSE$: the OLS problem.
  2. Definition 2: right angle. $e\perp X$ means $X'e=0$: the normal equations, i.e. the F.O.C.

Both give $b=(X'X)^{-1}X'y$.

Derive OLS with no calculus (a nice exam answer)

$X'e=0\Rightarrow X'(y-Xb)=0\Rightarrow X'Xb=X'y\Rightarrow b=(X'X)^{-1}X'y$.

INTERACTIVE Drop y on the floor— ⊡ ✕

Try this. Move $b_1,b_2$: $Xb$ slides around on the floor and the amber stick changes length. It is shortest exactly when it is perpendicular, and then $X'e=(0,0)'$. Press exact fit: $y$ lies on the floor, $e=0$, $R^2=1$. Press worst fit: $y$ is perpendicular to the floor, $\hat y=0$, $e=y$, $R^2=0$. Those are the two slide questions of slide 12.

DEF The two projection matrices— ⊡ ✕
$$\hat y=Xb=\underbrace{X(X'X)^{-1}X'}_{P}\,y,\qquad e=\underbrace{(I_n-P)}_{M}\,y$$
$P$ (makes $\hat y$)$M$ (makes $e$)
symmetric✓✓
idempotent$PP=P$$MM=M$
on $X$$PX=X$$MX=0$
rank = trace$K$$n-K$
each other$PM=0$$P+M=I_n$

Meaning. $PX=X$: a column of $X$ is already on the floor, so its shadow is itself. $MX=0$: regress $X$ on $X$ and nothing is left over. $PM=0$: the shadow of something vertical is zero. $PP=P$: the shadow of a shadow is the same shadow.

Word choice on slide 12 of lecture 4

The slide calls $M$ a "rotation" matrix. A rotation keeps lengths; $M$ shortens $y$ to $e$ ($\lVert e\rVert\le\lVert y\rVert$). In an exam say projection (onto the space orthogonal to $X$) or "residual maker".

LECTURE 3, SLIDE 10 · exercise: show that ŷ′e = 0

$\hat y'e=(Xb)'e=b'X'e=b'\cdot0=0$. $\blacksquare$ In words: $\hat y$ lies on the floor and $e$ is perpendicular to everything on the floor.

With the matrices: $\hat y'e=(Py)'(My)=y'P'My=y'PMy=0$.

PYTHAGORAS The decomposition behind $R^2$— ⊡ ✕

Since $y=\hat y+e$ and $\hat y'e=0$,

$$\lVert y\rVert^2=y'y=(\hat y+e)'(\hat y+e)=\hat y'\hat y+e'e=\lVert\hat y\rVert^2+\lVert e\rVert^2 .$$

It is Pythagoras' theorem in $\mathbb R^n$: $y$ is the hypotenuse.

Running example: $y'y=4+9+25+16+36=90$. $\hat y'\hat y=4.84+9.61+16+24.01+33.64=88.10$. $e'e=1.90$. Sum: $90$ ✓.

?●●practiceProject by hand

$y=(1,2,6)'$, $X$ = column of ones. Find $P$, $\hat y$, $e$.

Solution

$X'X=3$, $P=\frac13\mathbf1\mathbf1'$ (all entries $1/3$). $\hat y=(3,3,3)'$ since $\bar y=3$. $e=(-2,-1,3)'$, which sums to 0 ✓. Projecting on the constant = replacing by the mean; $M=I-\frac13\mathbf1\mathbf1'$ de-means.

?●●practiceWhat if you add a regressor already in the span?

You add $X_3=X_1+2X_2$. What happens to $\hat y$? To $b$?

Solution

The column space is unchanged, so $\hat y$ and $e$ are unchanged. But $b$ is no longer unique: infinitely many combinations give the same $\hat y$. $X'X$ is singular. The projection always exists; the coefficients need A1b.

?●●●practiceRegress residuals

Regress $e$ on $X$. What are the coefficients? Regress $\hat y$ on $X$. What are the coefficients?

Solution

$(X'X)^{-1}X'e=0$: nothing left. $(X'X)^{-1}X'\hat y=(X'X)^{-1}X'Xb=b$, with zero residuals.
