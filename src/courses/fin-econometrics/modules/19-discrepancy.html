The testing procedure and the discrepancy vector

lecture 5, slides 14–20
READ FIRST Is m close to zero? Close compared with what?— ⊡ ✕

We can't see $R\beta-q$, so we estimate it by $m=Rb-q$. If $m$ is close to zero, fine; if far, reject. Two problems: $m$ is a vector, and $m$ is random. The solution is to measure its length in units of its own standard deviation, and to compare that length with the values it would typically take if $H_0$ were true.

LOGIC Two problems, one fix— ⊡ ✕
problemfix
1) $m$ is $J\times1$. What does "a vector is close to zero" mean?take a norm: a single number. Use the standardized quadratic form $m'(\mathbb V[m\mid X])^{-1}m$, which is $1\times1$.
2) $m$ is random. What does "a random variable is close to zero" mean?find its distribution under $H_0$. It tells us which values are plausible if $H_0$ is true.
  1. Derive the distribution of $m=Rb-q$ under $H_0$ (before seeing data).
  2. Measure it with $m'(\mathbb V[m\mid X])^{-1}m$, a scalar.
  3. Derive the distribution of that scalar under $H_0$.
  4. With the data $\{y,X\}$, compute $b$, then $m$, then the scalar.
  5. Compare: a plausible value ⇒ do not reject; an implausible one ⇒ reject.
KEY The chain of distributions (assume $\varepsilon\mid X\sim N(0,\sigma^2I_n)$)— ⊡ ✕

Mean of $m$: $\mathbb E[m\mid X]=R\,\mathbb E[b\mid X]-q=R\beta-q\ \overset{H_0}{=}\ 0$

Variance of $m$: $\mathbb V[m\mid X]=R\,\mathbb V[b\mid X]\,R'=\sigma^2R(X'X)^{-1}R'$

$$\boxed{m\mid X\ \overset{H_0}{\sim}\ N\big(0,\ \sigma^2R(X'X)^{-1}R'\big)}$$
Two things decide the distribution of $m$

(1) The distribution of $\varepsilon$. (2) Whether $H_0$ is true. If it is false, $\mathbb E[m\mid X]=R\beta-q\neq0$ and $m$ tends to be far from zero. That contrast is what makes testing possible.

First time we assume normality

Everything in lectures 1–4 (meaning of the model, OLS, unbiased, variance, BLUE, $s^2$) holds for any reasonable distribution of $\varepsilon$. From here on the normal is assumed, only to carry out tests.

INTERACTIVE Why plain length is not enough— ⊡ ✕

Try this. Press the button repeatedly: $m$ flips between $(1,1)'$ and $(1,-1)'$. Both have Euclidean length 1.41, but one lies along the cloud and the other across it. Only the one across the cloud is surprising under $H_0$. $m'V^{-1}m$ knows the shape of the cloud; $m'm$ does not. In one dimension it reduces to $(m/\text{sd})^2$.

RUNNING EXAMPLE · m and its variance (σ² = 1 known, for illustration)

$H_0:\beta_1=0,\ \beta_2=1$. $R=I_2$, $q=(0,1)'$, $m=(1.3,\,-0.1)'$, $V=\sigma^2(X'X)^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1.1&-0.3\\-0.3&0.1\end{bmatrix}$, $V^{-1}=X'X$.

$$m'V^{-1}m=[1.3\ \ {-0.1}]\begin{bmatrix}5&15\\15&55\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1.3\\-0.1\end{bmatrix}=[1.3\ \ {-0.1}]\begin{bmatrix}5\\14\end{bmatrix}=6.5-1.4=5.1$$

Under $H_0$ this is a draw from $\chi^2_2$, whose 95% quantile is 5.99. $5.1<5.99$: do not reject at 5%.

$H_0:\beta_2=\beta_3$. Write $m$ and $\mathbb V[m\mid X]$ in scalars.

Solution

$R=[0\ 1\ {-1}]$, $m=b_2-b_3$, $\mathbb V[m\mid X]=\mathbb V[b_2\mid X]+\mathbb V[b_3\mid X]-2\text{Cov}[b_2,b_3\mid X]=\sigma^2\big(v_{22}+v_{33}-2v_{23}\big)$ with $v=(X'X)^{-1}$.

?●●practiceUnder the alternative

$H_0:\beta_2=0$ but truly $\beta_2=0.5$, with $\mathbb V[b_2\mid X]=0.04$. What is the distribution of $m$, and of $m/\text{sd}(m)$?

Solution

$m=b_2\sim N(0.5,0.04)$; $m/0.2\sim N(2.5,1)$. So the statistic is centred at 2.5, not 0: it usually exceeds 1.96 (module 21 calls that probability power).
