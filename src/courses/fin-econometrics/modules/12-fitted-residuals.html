Fitted values, residuals & the DGP ↔ sample parallel

lecture 3, slides 2–5
READ FIRST Two parallel worlds— ⊡ ✕

On one side is the data generating process (DGP): true, unobserved. On the other is the sample version we compute. Every object on the left has a partner on the right. Keep the two columns apart and most conceptual exam questions answer themselves.

Before and after

Before observing the sample, $b$ is a random variable: $\varepsilon$ random $\Rightarrow$ $y$ random $\Rightarrow$ $b$ random. After observing it, $b$ is a realization: numbers. $\beta$ is a constant before and after.

TRAP · $X'e=0$ proves nothing about exogeneity

The residuals are orthogonal to $X$ by construction, even when the errors are badly correlated with $X$. You cannot test A2a by looking at the correlation between $e$ and $X$: it is always exactly zero.

Three zeros to check every time:

  • $\sum e_i=0$: the constant's normal equation. Hence $\overline{\hat y}=\bar y$.
  • $\sum x_ie_i=0$: the regressor's normal equation.
  • $\hat y'e=\sum\hat y_ie_i=0$: follows from the other two, since $\hat y$ is a combination of the columns (module 13).

If your hand computation fails one of these, there is an arithmetic mistake.

FIGURE Errors and residuals for the same point— ⊡ ✕

How are they related? $e=y-Xb=X\beta+\varepsilon-Xb=\varepsilon-X(b-\beta)$. The residual is the error minus the estimation mistake. In module 17: $e=M\varepsilon$.

With $b=(0.9,1.4)'$ and $x=(0,1,2,3)$, $y=(1,3,2,6)$ (module 09), compute $\hat y'e$.

Solution

$\hat y=(0.9,2.3,3.7,5.1)$, $e=(0.1,0.7,-1.7,0.9)$. $0.09+1.61-6.29+4.59=0$ ✓.

?●●practiceWhat would change?

The researcher draws a new sample from the same DGP. Which change: $\beta$, $b$, $\varepsilon$, $e$, $X'e$, $\sigma^2$, $s^2$?

Solution

Change: $b,\varepsilon,e,s^2$. Don't change: $\beta,\sigma^2$. $X'e$ is 0 in every sample.
