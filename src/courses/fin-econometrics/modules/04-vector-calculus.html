Vector differentiation

Homework 2, ex. 1 · the two rules behind OLS
READ FIRST Two derivatives and you can derive OLS— ⊡ ✕

Minimising the sum of squared errors needs the derivative of a scalar with respect to a vector. Only two rules matter: the derivative of a linear form $a'b$ and of a quadratic form $b'Ab$.

RULES The two rules— ⊡ ✕

$f(b)$ is a scalar, $b$ is $K\times1$. The gradient stacks the partial derivatives, so it is $K\times1$ too:

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial b}=\begin{bmatrix}\partial f/\partial b_1\\\vdots\\\partial f/\partial b_K\end{bmatrix}$$ $$\boxed{\frac{\partial\,a'b}{\partial b}=a\qquad\frac{\partial\,b'Ab}{\partial b}=(A+A')b}$$

If $A$ is symmetric: $\dfrac{\partial\,b'Ab}{\partial b}=2Ab$. Compare with $\frac{d}{db}ab^2=2ab$.

Why. $a'b=\sum_ka_kb_k$, so $\partial/\partial b_k=a_k$. For the quadratic form, $b'Ab=\sum_j\sum_kA_{jk}b_jb_k$. The variable $b_k$ appears once as "row" and once as "column", so

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial b_k}=\sum_jA_{kj}b_j+\sum_jA_{jk}b_j=[Ab]_k+[A'b]_k .$$
Used in lecture 2

$SSE=y'y-2y'Xb+b'X'Xb$. Linear part: $a'=-2y'X$, so $a=-2X'y$. Quadratic part: $A=X'X$ is symmetric, so $2X'Xb$. Together $\frac{\partial SSE}{\partial b}=-2X'y+2X'Xb$.

HOMEWORK 2 · EX 1.2 · part b) with a non-symmetric A

$A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\3&2\end{bmatrix}$, $b=\begin{bmatrix}b_1\\b_2\end{bmatrix}$, $f(b)=b'Ab$. Compute $\frac{df}{db}$.

Way 1: expand.

$$f=\begin{bmatrix}b_1&b_2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}2b_1+b_2\\3b_1+2b_2\end{bmatrix}=2b_1^2+b_1b_2+3b_1b_2+2b_2^2=2b_1^2+4b_1b_2+2b_2^2$$ $$\frac{df}{db}=\begin{bmatrix}4b_1+4b_2\\4b_1+4b_2\end{bmatrix}=4\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}b_1\\b_2\end{bmatrix}$$

Way 2: the rule.

$$(A+A')b=\left(\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\3&2\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}2&3\\1&2\end{bmatrix}\right)b=\begin{bmatrix}4&4\\4&4\end{bmatrix}b=4\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}b\ ✓$$
TRAP

Using $2Ab$ here gives $\begin{bmatrix}4b_1+2b_2\\6b_1+4b_2\end{bmatrix}$, which is wrong. $2Ab$ needs a symmetric $A$.

INTERACTIVE A quadratic form and its gradient— ⊡ ✕

The gradient always crosses the contour at a right angle, uphill. At a minimum it vanishes: that is the F.O.C. With a positive definite $A$ the contours are ellipses around a unique minimum, as for $SSE$ when $\text{rank}(X)=K$. With the singular matrix of Homework 2, $f=2(b_1+b_2)^2$ is a valley: a whole line of minima. A regression with perfectly collinear regressors looks like this: infinitely many $b$ fit equally well. With an indefinite matrix the F.O.C. finds a saddle, not a minimum. That is why lecture 2 also checks the S.O.C.

?●practiceLinear form

$a=(3,-1,2)'$. Find $\partial(a'b)/\partial b$ and $\partial(b'a)/\partial b$.

Solution

Both equal $a=(3,-1,2)'$, since $a'b=b'a$ is the same scalar.

?●●practiceA full OLS objective

$y=(1,3)'$, $X=(1,2)'$ (one regressor, no constant). Write $SSE(b)$, its derivative, and the minimiser.

Solution

$SSE=(1-b)^2+(3-2b)^2=10-14b+5b^2$. Matrix check: $y'y=10$, $X'y=7$, $X'X=5$ ✓. $SSE'=-14+10b=0\Rightarrow b=1.4=7/5=(X'X)^{-1}X'y$. $SSE''=10=2X'X>0$: minimum.
