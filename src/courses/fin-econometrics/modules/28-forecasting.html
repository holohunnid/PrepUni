Conditional vs unconditional, and forecasting

time series deck, slides 7–9
READ FIRST Do you know today's value, or not?— ⊡ ✕

Unconditional: you know the process but nothing about where it is now. Conditional: you stand at time $t$ and know $y_t$. Forecasting is conditional. The distinction is the exam favourite of this deck.

Conditional variance ≤ unconditional variance: knowing yesterday reduces uncertainty (strictly, unless $\phi_1=0$). Check with the law of total variance: $\mathbb V[Y_t]=\mathbb E[\sigma_\varepsilon^2]+\mathbb V[\phi_0+\phi_1Y_{t-1}]=\sigma_\varepsilon^2+\phi_1^2\mathbb V[Y_t]$ ✓.

FORECAST $h$ steps ahead— ⊡ ✕
$$\mathbb E[Y_{t+1}\mid Y_t=y_t]=\phi_0+\phi_1y_t$$ $$\mathbb E[Y_{t+2}\mid y_t]=\phi_0+\phi_1(\phi_0+\phi_1y_t)=\phi_0(1+\phi_1)+\phi_1^2y_t$$ $$\mathbb E[Y_{t+3}\mid y_t]=\phi_0(1+\phi_1+\phi_1^2)+\phi_1^3y_t$$

General rule:

$$\boxed{\mathbb E[Y_{t+h}\mid y_t]=\phi_0\frac{1-\phi_1^h}{1-\phi_1}+\phi_1^hy_t=\mu+\phi_1^h(y_t-\mu)}$$

The last form is the one to remember: long-run mean plus a fading share of today's deviation from it.

$$\lim_{h\to\infty}\mathbb E[Y_{t+h}\mid y_t]=\frac{\phi_0}{1-\phi_1}=\mu$$

Slide question: what's special? The limit does not depend on today's value $y_t$, and equals the unconditional mean. Far enough ahead, knowing today is worthless: mean reversion.

Forecast error variance (beyond the slide, same logic):

$$\mathbb V[Y_{t+h}\mid y_t]=\sigma_\varepsilon^2(1+\phi_1^2+\dots+\phi_1^{2(h-1)})=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\frac{1-\phi_1^{2h}}{1-\phi_1^2}\ \to\ \mathbb V[Y_t]$$
INTERACTIVE Forecast at work (slide 9: $\phi_0=0,\ \phi_1=0.5$)— ⊡ ✕

Try this. On the slide $y_{25}\approx1.7$ and the forecasts are $0.85, 0.43, 0.21, 0.11,\dots$: halved at each step. Raise $\phi_1$ to 0.95: the forecast comes back slowly. Set it to 1: the forecast is flat at $y_t$ for ever and the band keeps growing with no limit. The actual future zig-zags; the forecast is its mean, so it is smooth.

?●practiceForecast three steps

$Y_t=2+0.6Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t$, $\sigma_\varepsilon^2=4$, $y_t=10$. Forecast $h=1,2,3$ and $h=\infty$. Give the variance for $h=1,2$.

Solution

$\mu=5$. $5+0.6\cdot5=8$; $5+0.36\cdot5=6.8$; $5+0.216\cdot5=6.08$; limit 5. Variances: $4$; $4(1+0.36)=5.44$ (limit 6.25).

?●●practiceAn interest-rate model

Monthly short rate: $r_t=0.1+0.95r_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t$ (percent). Today $r=1\%$. Expected rate in 12 months? Long-run rate? Half-life of a deviation?

Solution

$\mu=0.1/0.05=2$. $2+0.95^{12}(1-2)=2-0.540=1.46\%$. Long run 2%. Half-life $\ln0.5/\ln0.95=13.5$ months.

?●●practiceProbability statement

With $Y_t=2+0.6Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t$, $\sigma_\varepsilon^2=4$: find $P(Y_{t+1}>9\mid y_t=10)$ and $P(Y_{t+1}>9)$.

Solution

Conditional: $N(8,4)$: $P(Z>0.5)=0.309$. Unconditional: $N(5,6.25)$: $P(Z>1.6)=0.055$. Today's high value makes a high tomorrow far more likely.
