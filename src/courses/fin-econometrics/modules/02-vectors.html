Independent and orthogonal are not the same

Orthogonal non-zero vectors are always independent. Independent vectors need not be orthogonal: they only have to point in different directions. Orthogonality is a right angle, independence is any angle other than 0° or 180°.

Inner product and length

$x'x=\sum x_i^2=\lVert x\rVert^2$ is the squared length. The angle satisfies $\cos\theta=\dfrac{x_1'x_2}{\lVert x_1\rVert\,\lVert x_2\rVert}$. If both vectors are de-meaned, $\cos\theta$ is the sample correlation.

EXERCISE SET 1 · all five parts

Consider $x_1=\begin{bmatrix}2\\3\end{bmatrix}$ and $x_2=\begin{bmatrix}-1\\2\end{bmatrix}$ in $\mathbb R^2$.

  1. Draw them. Arrows from $(0,0)$ to $(2,3)$ and from $(0,0)$ to $(-1,2)$ (figure below).
  2. Independent? Yes. Neither is a multiple of the other: $-1/2\neq2/3$. Equivalently $\det\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\3&2\end{bmatrix}=2\cdot2-(-1)\cdot3=7\neq0$.
  3. Orthogonal? No: $x_1'x_2=2\cdot(-1)+3\cdot2=4\neq0$.
  4. Basis of $\mathbb R^2$? Yes: two independent vectors in a space of dimension 2.
  1. Find $b$ with $\hat y=x_1b_1+x_2b_2$ for $\hat y=(1,-3)'$. Write it as a system and invert: $$\begin{bmatrix}1\\-3\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\3&2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}b_1\\b_2\end{bmatrix}\ \Rightarrow\ b=\frac17\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\-3&2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1\\-3\end{bmatrix}=\frac17\begin{bmatrix}-1\\-9\end{bmatrix}$$ so $b_1=-\frac17=-0.1429$, $b_2=-\frac97=-1.2857$.
  2. Check. $2(-0.1429)-(-1.2857)=1$ ✓ and $3(-0.1429)+2(-1.2857)=-3$ ✓.

?●check yourselfIndependent or not?

Are $(1,2,3)'$ and $(2,4,6)'$ independent? And $(1,1,1)'$ and $(1,2,3)'$? Do the last two form a basis of $\mathbb R^3$?

Solution

The first pair is dependent (the second is twice the first). The second pair is independent. It is not a basis of $\mathbb R^3$: two vectors span only a plane. In regression terms: with $n=3$ and $K=2$ the column space of $X$ is a plane inside $\mathbb R^3$, and $y$ is usually off it.

?●●check yourselfThe dummy-variable trap

$X$ has three columns: ones, a dummy for "January to June" and a dummy for "July to December". Is $\text{rank}(X)=3$?

Solution

No. The two dummies add up to the column of ones, so $X_1-X_2-X_3=0$ is a non-trivial combination equal to zero. $\text{rank}(X)=2$, $X'X$ is singular and OLS has no unique solution. Drop one dummy (or the constant).
