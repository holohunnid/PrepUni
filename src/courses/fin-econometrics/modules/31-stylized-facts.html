Asset returns: the stylized facts

GARCH deck, slides 2–10
READ FIRST What all asset returns have in common— ⊡ ✕

Equities, bonds, commodities and currencies differ economically, but their returns share the same statistical regularities. These stylized facts are (essentially) model-free and any good model must reproduce them. Memorise the list with one sentence of evidence for each.

The key sentence about dependence

Returns are (nearly) uncorrelated but not independent: $r_t$ does not help to predict $r_{t+1}$, but $r_t^2$ helps to predict $r_{t+1}^2$. The direction is unpredictable, the size is predictable. Compare module 22: zero covariance is not independence.

DEF Log-returns, and why equity is asymmetric— ⊡ ✕

Log-return: $r_t=\log(S_t/S_{t-1})$. Close to the simple return for small moves ($\log(1+x)\approx x$). Multi-day log-returns add up: $\log(S_{t+2}/S_t)=r_{t+1}+r_{t+2}$.

A standardized return is $(r_t-\bar r)/\hat\sigma$. Annualize daily volatility with $\sqrt{252}$: 1% per day is about 15.9% per year.

Merton's view of the firm (slide 2). Debt has face value $D$, assets are worth $A$ at maturity.

$$\text{Equity}=\max(A-D,0)\qquad\text{Debt}=\min(A,D)$$

Equity is a call option on the firm's assets; debt holders own the assets and are short a put. So different claims on the same assets have very different payoffs. It also suggests why volatility rises when prices fall: with lower $A$ the firm is more leveraged, so equity becomes riskier (the "leverage" in leverage effect).

INTERACTIVE Can an i.i.d. normal model produce the facts?— ⊡ ✕

Try this. i.i.d. normal: no clusters, both ACFs flat, histogram on the curve. It fails every fact. GARCH: clusters appear, the ACF of $r_t^2$ is positive, tails get heavier (kurtosis above 3) even though the shocks are normal, while the ACF of $r_t$ stays flat. With $t$ shocks the tails are heavier still. What neither has: the leverage effect (module 35 adds it).

?●practiceLog and simple

The index falls from 100 to 79.5 in one day. Simple and log return? If the daily sd is 1.12%, what is the standardized log-return?

Solution

Simple −20.5%; log $\ln0.795=-22.9\%$. Standardized: $-22.9/1.12=-20.5$, the deck's number for the 1987 crash. Note how the two returns differ for large moves.

?●●practiceUncorrelated, not independent

$r_t=\sigma_tz_t$ with $\sigma_t$ known at $t-1$ and $z_t$ i.i.d. mean 0. Show $\text{Cov}[r_t,r_{t-1}]=0$ but that $r_t^2$ and $r_{t-1}^2$ can be correlated.

Solution

$\mathbb E[r_tr_{t-1}]=\mathbb E\big[r_{t-1}\sigma_t\mathbb E[z_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\big]=0$. But $\mathbb E[r_t^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=\sigma_t^2$, which may depend on $r_{t-1}^2$ (ARCH does exactly this), so the squares are related.
