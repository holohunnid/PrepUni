MOCK A Asset pricing with two factors (30 points)— ⊡ ✕

For $t=1,\dots,60$ months: $r_t^{ex}=\beta_1+\beta_2f_{t}+\beta_3g_{t}+\varepsilon_t$, where $f_t,g_t$ are factor returns. $\varepsilon_t$ i.i.d. $(0,\sigma^2)$, independent of $(f_t,g_t)$. OLS gives $b=(0.2,\ 1.1,\ 0.4)'$, $e'e=114$ and

$$(X'X)^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}0.02&0&0\\0&0.010&-0.005\\0&-0.005&0.020\end{bmatrix}$$
?●A1 · 4 pointsMatrix form

Write the model as $y=X\beta+\varepsilon$ with content and dimensions.

Solution

$y$: $60\times1$ excess returns; $X$: $60\times3$ with rows $(1,f_t,g_t)$; $\beta=(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3)'$; $\varepsilon$: $60\times1$. [1 point each]

?●●A2 · 6 pointsProperties

(a) Is $b$ unbiased? Prove it. (b) Would it still be unbiased if $\mathbb V[\varepsilon_t]$ were larger in crises? (c) Would it still be BLUE?

Solution

(a) [3] $\mathbb E[b\mid X]=(X'X)^{-1}X'\mathbb E[y\mid X]=\beta$ using $\mathbb E[\varepsilon\mid X]=0$; tower property. (b) [1.5] Yes: the proof uses only the mean. (c) [1.5] No: A2b fails, Gauss–Markov does not apply.

?●●A3 · 5 pointsEstimate σ² and standard errors

Compute $s^2$, se of each coefficient, and $\widehat{\text{Cov}}[b_2,b_3\mid X]$.

Solution

$s^2=114/57=2$ [2]. se: $\sqrt{0.04}=0.2$; $\sqrt{0.02}=0.141$; $\sqrt{0.04}=0.2$ [2]. Cov $=2\cdot(-0.005)=-0.01$ [1].

?●●A4 · 10 pointsTests at 5% (normal errors)

$t_{57,0.975}=2.00$, $t_{57,0.95}=1.67$, $\bar F_{2,57}=3.16$. (a) $H_0:\beta_1=0$ vs $H_A:\beta_1>0$. (b) $H_0:\beta_2=1$ vs $\neq$. (c) $H_0:\beta_2+\beta_3=1$ vs $\neq$. (d) $H_0:\beta_2=1,\beta_3=0$.

Solution

(a) [2] $t=0.2/0.2=1.00<1.67$ ⇒ do not reject: no significant alpha. (b) [2] $t=0.1/0.141=0.71$ ⇒ do not reject. (c) [3] $R=[0\,1\,1]$, $m=0.5$, $R(X'X)^{-1}R'=0.010+0.020-0.010=0.020$, se $=\sqrt{0.04}=0.2$, $t=2.5>2.00$ ⇒ reject. (d) [3] $m=(0.1,0.4)'$, block inverse: $\det=0.0002-0.000025=0.000175$; inverse $=\frac1{0.000175}\begin{bmatrix}0.020&0.005\\0.005&0.010\end{bmatrix}$. $m'(\cdot)m=\frac{0.020\cdot0.01+2\cdot0.005\cdot0.04+0.010\cdot0.16}{0.000175}=\frac{0.0002+0.0004+0.0016}{0.000175}=12.57$. $F=\frac{12.57}{2\cdot2}=3.14<3.16$ ⇒ do not reject (just).

?●●A5 · 5 pointsDiscuss

Your colleague adds 20 more factors and reports that $R^2$ rose from 0.55 to 0.70. Is the new model better? Give a test.

Solution

[2] $R^2$ never falls when regressors are added. [3] F-test with $J=20$, $n-K=60-23=37$: $F=\frac{0.15/20}{0.30/37}=\frac{0.0075}{0.00811}=0.93$, below any usual critical value (about 1.9): the 20 factors are not jointly significant.

MOCK B Theory and time series (30 points)— ⊡ ✕
?●B1 · 6 pointsYES / NO with one sentence

(a) $X'e=0$ shows that the regressors are exogenous. (b) If $n$ doubles and the spread of $x$ is unchanged, the standard error of the slope falls by about 29%. (c) A 5% test rejects a true null in 5% of samples. (d) OLS residuals are homoskedastic if errors are. (e) The $F$-test of $\beta_2=0$ can be used against $\beta_2>0$. (f) $\text{Cov}=0$ implies independence for jointly normal variables.

Solution

(a) NO, it holds by construction. (b) YES, factor $1/\sqrt2=0.707$. (c) YES, definition of size. (d) NO, $\mathbb V[e\mid X]=\sigma^2M$. (e) NO, $F=t^2$ loses the sign. (f) YES.

?●●●B2 · 8 pointsA rival estimator

In $y_i=\beta x_i+\varepsilon_i$ (A1–A2 hold) consider $\tilde b=\frac1n\sum\frac{y_i}{x_i}$, $x_i\neq0$. (a) Linear? (b) Unbiased? (c) Variance. (d) Compare with OLS for $x=(1,2)$. (e) Which theorem predicts the result?

Solution

(a) [1] Yes: weights $\frac1{nx_i}$. (b) [2] $\mathbb E=\frac1n\sum\frac{\beta x_i}{x_i}=\beta$. (c) [2] $\frac{\sigma^2}{n^2}\sum\frac1{x_i^2}$. (d) [2] $\frac{\sigma^2}4(1+\frac14)=0.3125\sigma^2$ vs OLS $\frac{\sigma^2}{5}=0.2\sigma^2$. (e) [1] Gauss–Markov.

?●●B3 · 10 pointsAR(1)

$Y_t=1+0.8Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t$, $\varepsilon_t\sim$ i.i.d. $N(0,0.36)$. (a) Stationary? Unconditional mean, variance, distribution. (b) $y_t=9$: conditional mean and variance of $Y_{t+1}$; $\mathbb E[Y_{t+3}\mid y_t]$; limit. (c) $\rho(1),\rho(4)$. (d) 95% interval for $Y_{t+1}$ given $y_t=9$. (e) Repeat (b)'s forecasts if $\phi_1=1,\phi_0=0$.

Solution

(a) [3] Yes, $\lvert0.8\rvert<1$. Mean 5, variance $0.36/0.36=1$, $N(5,1)$. (b) [3] $8.2$; $0.36$; $5+0.512\cdot4=7.048$; limit 5. (c) [1] $0.8$; $0.4096$. (d) [1.5] $8.2\pm1.96\cdot0.6=[7.02,9.38]$. (e) [1.5] 9 at every horizon; variance $0.36h$.

?●●B4 · 6 pointsEstimate it

You observe $Y_1,\dots,Y_{121}$. (a) Write the AR(1) as $y=X\beta+\varepsilon$ with dimensions. (b) $\hat\phi_1=0.85$, se $0.05$. Test $\phi_1=1$ with the usual $t$ and say why the result should be taken with caution.

Solution

(a) [3] $y=(Y_2,\dots,Y_{121})'$: $120\times1$; $X$: $120\times2$, rows $(1,Y_{t-1})$; $\beta=(\phi_0,\phi_1)'$; $\varepsilon$: $120\times1$. (b) [3] $t=(0.85-1)/0.05=-3$: would reject with $t_{118}$. Caution: under $\phi_1=1$ the process is not stationary, LLN/CLT do not apply in the usual way and the statistic is not $t$-distributed; moreover $\hat\phi_1$ is biased downwards.

MOCK C Testing, GARCH and risk (30 points)— ⊡ ✕
?●●C1 · 8 pointsJoint test, σ² known

$y_i=\beta_1+\beta_2x_{i2}+\beta_3x_{i3}+\varepsilon_i$, $\varepsilon\mid X\sim N(0,4I)$. $b=(1,2,-1)'$, $(X'X)^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}0.5&0&0\\0&0.25&0\\0&0&0.125\end{bmatrix}$. Test $H_0:\beta_1=0,\ \beta_2+\beta_3=0$ at 5%. State $R,q$, the statistic, its distribution, the value, the decision, and draw the pdf.

Solution

[2] $R=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&1\end{bmatrix}$, $q=(0,0)'$. [2] $\chi^2_2$ statistic, since $\sigma^2$ known and $J=2$. [3] $m=(1,1)'$; $R(X'X)^{-1}R'=\text{diag}(0.5,0.375)$; $\times4$: $\text{diag}(2,1.5)$; value $\frac12+\frac1{1.5}=1.167$. [1] $<5.99$ ⇒ do not reject; density decreasing from 0.5, right tail 5% beyond 5.99.

?●●C2 · 7 pointsSkill or luck

A bank tests 400 traders for positive alpha at 1%. 9 are significant. (a) Expected number if nobody has skill? (b) Is 9 surprising? Use the normal approximation. (c) The bank wants to pay a bonus to the 9. Advise. (d) What happens to the number of rejections, and to Type II errors, at 0.1%?

Solution

(a) [1] 4. (b) [2] sd $\sqrt{3.96}=1.99$, $z=2.5$: more than luck alone, so there is probably some skill. (c) [2] About 4 of the 9 are probably lucky; you can't tell which. Use a longer sample or out-of-sample performance. (d) [2] Fewer rejections (0.4 expected under no skill); Type II errors rise: genuinely skilled traders are missed.

?●●C3 · 9 pointsGARCH, VaR, ES

$\sigma_t^2=0.04+0.88\sigma_{t-1}^2+0.08r_{t-1}^2$ (percent), $z\sim N(0,1)$. Today $\sigma_t^2=2$, $r_t=-4$. (a) Long-run variance, persistence. (b) $\sigma_{t+1}^2$. (c) 1% one-day VaR and ES in percent and for 2 million. (d) Expected variance at $t+2$. (e) Which stylized fact does the model miss, and how would you extend it?

Solution

(a) [2] $0.04/0.04=1$; $0.96$. (b) [1.5] $0.04+1.76+1.28=3.08$. (c) [3] $\sigma=1.755$. VaR $2.326\cdot1.755=4.08\%$ ⇒ 81,600; ES $2.665\cdot1.755=4.68\%$ ⇒ 93,500. (d) [1] $1+0.96(3.08-1)=2.997$. (e) [1.5] Leverage effect: add $\beta_3I_{t-1}r_{t-1}^2$ (GJR).

?●●C4 · 6 pointsLikelihood and diagnostics

(a) Write the Gaussian log-likelihood of C3's model. (b) Why not OLS? (c) Name two checks on $\hat z_t$. (d) The 1% quantile of $\hat z$ is $-2.8$. What does it imply for (c) of C3?

Solution

(a) [2] $-\frac12\sum_t(\log2\pi+\log\sigma_t^2+r_t^2/\sigma_t^2)$. (b) [1] $\sigma_t^2$ is not observed. (c) [1.5] ACF of $\hat z_t^2$ flat; variance about 1 / no clusters / normality of tails. (d) [1.5] Tails heavier than normal: VaR should be $2.8\cdot1.755=4.91\%$, not 4.08%.
