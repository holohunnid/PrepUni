Autocorrelation, stationarity & the random walk

time series deck, slides 10–12 · GARCH deck, slide 8
READ FIRST How long does the series remember?— ⊡ ✕

The autocorrelation function (ACF) measures the correlation between the series and its own past, lag by lag. For an AR(1) it decays geometrically, $\rho(h)=\phi_1^h$. Stationarity means the mean and variance do not change over time. It is what makes averaging over time meaningful, and the random walk is the standard example of it failing.

DERIVATION The ACF of an AR(1)— ⊡ ✕

Set $\phi_0=0$, so $\mathbb E[Y_t]=0$; $\lvert\phi_1\rvert<1$.

  1. $\text{Cov}[Y_{t+1},Y_t]=\mathbb E[Y_{t+1}Y_t]$ (means are zero)
  2. $=\mathbb E\big[\mathbb E[Y_{t+1}Y_t\mid Y_t]\big]$ (tower)
  3. $=\mathbb E\big[\mathbb E[(\phi_1Y_t+\varepsilon_{t+1})Y_t\mid Y_t]\big]=\mathbb E[\phi_1Y_t^2+Y_t\cdot0]$
  4. $=\phi_1\mathbb E[Y_t^2]=\phi_1\mathbb V[Y_t]$
  5. After recursive substitutions $\text{Cov}[Y_{t+h},Y_t]=\phi_1^h\,\mathbb V[Y_t]$
$$\boxed{\rho(h)=\frac{\text{Cov}[Y_{t+h},Y_t]}{\sqrt{\mathbb V[Y_{t+h}]\mathbb V[Y_t]}}=\phi_1^h}$$

The last step uses stationarity: $\mathbb V[Y_{t+h}]=\mathbb V[Y_t]$.

Slide question: $\rho(0)=1$ (correlation with itself, $\phi_1^0$).

"Recursive substitutions", shown: $Y_{t+h}=\phi_1^hY_t+\varepsilon_{t+h}+\phi_1\varepsilon_{t+h-1}+\dots+\phi_1^{h-1}\varepsilon_{t+1}$. All those shocks come after $t$ and are independent of $Y_t$, so only $\phi_1^hY_t$ covaries.

Sample version (GARCH deck; mean 0, variance 1):

$$ACF(\ell)=\frac1{T-\ell}\sum_{t=1}^{T-\ell}y_ty_{t+\ell}$$

$ACF(0)$ is the sample variance, $ACF(1)$ the sample covariance between $y_t$ and $y_{t+1}$. Note the divisor: only $T-\ell$ pairs exist.

INTERACTIVE Theoretical and sample ACF— ⊡ ✕

Try this. With $\phi_1=0$ the bars stay inside the band about 95% of the time: that is the picture of daily returns. With $\phi_1\lt0$ the signs alternate. Small $T$: the sample ACF is noisy and biased downwards for persistent series.

DEF Weak stationarity— ⊡ ✕

A process is (weakly) stationary if its unconditional mean and variance do not depend on $t$ and exist. (The full textbook definition adds: $\text{Cov}[Y_{t+h},Y_t]$ depends only on $h$.)

The AR(1) with $\lvert\phi_1\rvert<1$ is stationary:

$$\mathbb E[Y_t]=\frac{\phi_0}{1-\phi_1},\qquad\mathbb V[Y_t]=\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{1-\phi_1^2}$$

Why we care.

  • Inference: the Law of Large Numbers and Central Limit Theorem apply, so time averages estimate moments.
  • Forecasting: the forecast converges to a stable mean.
  • Finance: prices are close to random walks (non-stationary); returns are close to stationary. Model returns.
RANDOM WALK "AR(1)" with $\phi_1=1$— ⊡ ✕
$$Y_t=Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t=Y_{t-h}+\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\varepsilon_{t-j}$$

Forecast: $\mathbb E[Y_{t+h}\mid Y_t=y_t]=y_t$ for every $h\ge1$. The best forecast at any horizon is today's value.

Conditional variance: $\mathbb V[Y_{t+h}\mid y_t]=h\sigma_\varepsilon^2$: grows without bound, sd grows like $\sqrt h$.

Slide question: $\mathbb E[Y_t]$ and $\mathbb V[Y_t]$? Starting from a fixed $Y_0$:

$$\mathbb E[Y_t]=Y_0,\qquad\mathbb V[Y_t]=t\,\sigma_\varepsilon^2$$

The variance depends on $t$: not stationary. With the infinite past of the slide, $Y_t=\sum_{j=0}^\infty\varepsilon_{t-j}$ has infinite variance and the unconditional mean is not well defined: the formulas $\frac{\phi_0}{1-\phi_1}$ and $\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{1-\phi_1^2}$ divide by zero.

Shocks never die: all Wold weights are 1.

INTERACTIVE Stationary or not? Fifty paths from the same start— ⊡ ✕

The fan of the random walk opens like $\sqrt t$ for ever. At $\phi_1=0.9$ it opens and then stops at $\sigma_\varepsilon/\sqrt{1-0.81}=2.29\,\sigma_\varepsilon$. At 0.99 it looks like a random walk for a long time: near-unit-root series are hard to tell apart from random walks in short samples.

$y=(1,-1,2,0,-2)$, treat mean as 0. Compute $ACF(0)$ and $ACF(1)$ with the deck's formula, and the lag-1 autocorrelation.

Solution

$ACF(0)=\frac15(1+1+4+0+4)=2$. $ACF(1)=\frac14\big(1\cdot(-1)+(-1)\cdot2+2\cdot0+0\cdot(-2)\big)=-\frac34$. Since the variance is not 1, the autocorrelation is $-0.75/2=-0.375$.
