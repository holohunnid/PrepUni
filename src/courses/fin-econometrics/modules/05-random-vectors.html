READ FIRST Four rules that do almost all the probability in this course— ⊡ ✕

$\varepsilon$, $y$, $b$, $e$, $m$ are all random vectors. To find their mean and variance you need two rules for linear functions, the tower property, and one fact about the normal.

RULES Means and variances of vectors— ⊡ ✕

For a random vector $Z$ ($n\times1$) and constant matrix $A$, vector $c$:

$$\boxed{\mathbb E[AZ+c]=A\,\mathbb E[Z]+c\qquad\mathbb V[AZ+c]=A\,\mathbb V[Z]\,A'}$$ $$\mathbb V[Z]=\mathbb E\big[(Z-\mathbb E Z)(Z-\mathbb E Z)'\big]\quad(n\times n)$$

The diagonal of $\mathbb V[Z]$ holds variances, the off-diagonal covariances. It is symmetric and non-negative definite. If $\mathbb E[Z]=0$, then $\mathbb V[Z]=\mathbb E[ZZ']$.

$$\text{Cov}[Z,W]=\mathbb E\big[(Z-\mathbb EZ)(W-\mathbb EW)'\big],\qquad\text{Cov}[AZ,BW]=A\,\text{Cov}[Z,W]\,B'$$
TRAP · $ZZ'$ and $Z'Z$

$ZZ'$ is an $n\times n$ matrix. $Z'Z=\sum Z_i^2$ is a number. $\mathbb E[\varepsilon\varepsilon']=\sigma^2I_n$ (matrix) while $\mathbb E[\varepsilon'\varepsilon]=n\sigma^2$ (number). They are linked by the trace: $\varepsilon'\varepsilon=\text{tr}(\varepsilon\varepsilon')$.

The scalar version you know

$\mathbb V[aZ+c]=a^2\mathbb V[Z]$ is the $1\times1$ case of $A\mathbb V[Z]A'$. And for two entries, $\mathbb V[c_1Z_1+c_2Z_2]=c_1^2\mathbb V_1+c_2^2\mathbb V_2+2c_1c_2\text{Cov}_{12}$ is $[c_1\ c_2]\,\mathbb V[Z]\,[c_1\ c_2]'$.

KEY Conditioning and the tower property— ⊡ ✕

"Conditional on $X$" means: treat $X$ as known numbers ("freeze $X$"). Any function of $X$ comes out of the expectation:

$$\mathbb E[g(X)Z\mid X]=g(X)\,\mathbb E[Z\mid X].$$

Tower property (law of iterated expectations):

$$\boxed{\mathbb E[Z]=\mathbb E\big[\mathbb E[Z\mid X]\big]}$$

If the inner expectation does not depend on $X$ (as in $\mathbb E[b\mid X]=\beta$), the outer one changes nothing: $\mathbb E[b]=\beta$.

Where it is used

  • L1: $\mathbb E[y\mid X]=X\beta$.
  • L4: $\mathbb E[b]=\mathbb E[\mathbb E[b\mid X]]=\beta$ and $\mathbb E[e'e]=\sigma^2(n-K)$.
  • TS: $\mathbb E[Y_{t+1}Y_t]=\mathbb E[\mathbb E[Y_{t+1}Y_t\mid Y_t]]$.
  • GARCH: $\mathbb E[r_t]=\mathbb E[\mathbb E[\sigma_tz_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]]=\mathbb E[\sigma_t\cdot0]=0$.

Law of total variance: $\mathbb V[Z]=\mathbb E[\mathbb V[Z\mid X]]+\mathbb V[\mathbb E[Z\mid X]]$.

?●practiceVariance of a linear combination

$\mathbb V[Z]=\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\1&9\end{bmatrix}$. Find $\mathbb V[Z_1-2Z_2]$ with matrices and with the scalar formula.

Solution

$A=[1\ -2]$: $AV=[4-2,\ 1-18]=[2,-17]$, then $AVA'=2+34=36$. Scalar: $4+4\cdot9+2\cdot1\cdot(-2)\cdot1=36$ ✓.

?●●practiceConditional, then unconditional

$\mathbb E[Z\mid X]=2X$, $\mathbb V[Z\mid X]=X^2$, with $\mathbb E[X]=1$, $\mathbb V[X]=3$. Find $\mathbb E[Z]$ and $\mathbb V[Z]$.

Solution

$\mathbb E[Z]=\mathbb E[2X]=2$. $\mathbb E[X^2]=3+1=4$. $\mathbb V[Z]=\mathbb E[X^2]+\mathbb V[2X]=4+12=16$.

?●●●practice · Additional Exercises W3, Ex. 2Combining two unbiased estimators

$\hat\theta_1,\hat\theta_2$ are independent unbiased estimators of $\theta$ with different, finite, positive variances $v_1,v_2$. Which $\hat\theta=c_1\hat\theta_1+c_2\hat\theta_2$ has minimum variance among all unbiased linear combinations?

Solution
  1. Unbiased: $\mathbb E[\hat\theta]=(c_1+c_2)\theta=\theta$ for every $\theta$ requires $c_1+c_2=1$, so $c_2=1-c_1$.
  2. Variance (independence removes the covariance): $\mathbb V[\hat\theta]=c_1^2v_1+(1-c_1)^2v_2$.
  3. F.O.C.: $2c_1v_1-2(1-c_1)v_2=0\Rightarrow c_1=\dfrac{v_2}{v_1+v_2}$. S.O.C.: $2v_1+2v_2>0$ ✓.
  4. Nicer form: divide by $v_1v_2$: $c_1=\dfrac{1/v_1}{1/v_1+1/v_2}$ and $c_2=\dfrac{1/v_2}{1/v_1+1/v_2}$. Weights are proportional to inverse variances: the more accurate estimator gets the larger weight.
  5. Minimum variance: $\dfrac{v_1v_2}{v_1+v_2}$, smaller than both $v_1$ and $v_2$. It pays to use even the worse estimator a little. This is the same logic as Gauss–Markov and as portfolio diversification.
