W8 PART 1 · LINEAR MODEL · complete answer

$C_t=\gamma_0+\gamma_1I_t+\gamma_2r_t+\varepsilon_t$, $t=1,\dots,T$; $\varepsilon_t\sim\text{i.i.d.}(0,\sigma^2)$, distribution unknown, independent of $(I_t,r_t)'$.

  1. Matrix form. $y=(C_1,\dots,C_T)'$ is $T\times1$; $X$ is $T\times3$ with rows $(1,I_t,r_t)$; $\beta=(\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\gamma_2)'$ is $3\times1$; $\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_T)'$ is $T\times1$. Then $y=X\beta+\varepsilon$.
  2. BLUE. The OLS estimator $\hat\beta=(X'X)^{-1}X'y$. It is linear in $y$; unbiased since $\mathbb E[\varepsilon\mid X]=0$; and, since $\mathbb V[\varepsilon\mid X]=\sigma^2I_T$ and $\text{rank}(X)=3$, by the Gauss–Markov theorem no other linear unbiased estimator has a smaller covariance matrix (the difference is non-negative definite).
  3. Normal errors: would the answer change? NO. Gauss–Markov uses only the first two moments of $\varepsilon$ given $X$ and full rank. Normality is one special case.
  4. $\mathbb E[e]$. $\mathbb E[e\mid X]=\mathbb E[y\mid X]-X\mathbb E[\hat\beta\mid X]=X\beta-X\beta=0$, so $\mathbb E[e]=\mathbb E[\mathbb E[e\mid X]]=0_{T\times1}$. (Or $e=M\varepsilon$.)
W8 PART 2 · HYPOTHESIS TESTING · complete answer

$\varepsilon_t\sim\text{i.i.d. }N(0,\sigma^2)$, $\sigma^2$ known. Joint null: $\gamma_0=0$ and $\gamma_1=\gamma_2$.

  1. $H_0:R\beta=q$: $$\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&-1\end{bmatrix}}_{R\ (2\times3)}\begin{bmatrix}\gamma_0\\\gamma_1\\\gamma_2\end{bmatrix}=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\end{bmatrix}}_{q\ (2\times1)}$$ versus $H_A:R\beta\neq q$. $J=2$.
  2. Statistic. Let $b$ be OLS and $m=Rb-q$. Normality gives $b\mid X\sim N(\beta,\sigma^2(X'X)^{-1})$, so under $H_0$ $m\mid X\sim N(0,\sigma^2R(X'X)^{-1}R')$ and $$(Rb-q)'\big(\sigma^2R(X'X)^{-1}R'\big)^{-1}(Rb-q)\sim\chi^2_2 .$$ It can be computed because $\sigma^2$ is known. (Otherwise: $F_{2,T-3}$ with $s^2$.)
  1. Graph. Draw the $\chi^2_2$ density: starts at $0.5$ at zero and decreases. Mark the critical value $5.99$ (95% quantile). To the left: "do not reject $H_0$". To the right, shade 5%: "reject $H_0$". See the figure in module 24.
  2. Rule. Reject if the statistic exceeds $5.99$. Only large values are evidence against $H_0$, because the statistic is a squared length.
