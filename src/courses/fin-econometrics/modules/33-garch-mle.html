Estimating GARCH by maximum likelihood, and checking it

GARCH deck, slides 14–17
READ FIRST No closed form: search for the parameters that make the data most likely— ⊡ ✕

$\sigma_t^2$ is not observed, so there is no regression to run. But given parameters, we can compute $\sigma_t^2$ recursively for every day and ask how likely the observed returns are. Maximum likelihood picks the parameters with the highest likelihood, by numerical search.

DERIVATION The log-likelihood— ⊡ ✕
  1. Factorise the joint density into one-step conditionals: $f(r_1,\dots,r_T)=\prod_{t=1}^Tf(r_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})$.
  2. Take logs: $\log f(r_1,\dots,r_T)=\sum_{t=1}^T\log f(r_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})$.
  3. Assume $r_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}\sim N(0,\sigma_t^2)$: $f(r_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_t^2}}\exp\big(-\frac{r_t^2}{2\sigma_t^2}\big)$.
  4. Insert: $$\boxed{\log L(\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2)=-\frac12\sum_{t=1}^T\Big(\log(2\pi)+\log\sigma_t^2+\frac{r_t^2}{\sigma_t^2}\Big)}$$

Where are the parameters? Inside $\sigma_t^2=\beta_0+\beta_1\sigma_{t-1}^2+\beta_2r_{t-1}^2$.

Maximise numerically: no closed-form $\hat\beta_0,\hat\beta_1,\hat\beta_2$ in general.

Recursion needs a start: $\sigma_1^2$ (and $r_1^2$), e.g. the sample variance of the log-returns.

Trade-off inside each term: $\log\sigma_t^2$ penalises a variance that is too high; $r_t^2/\sigma_t^2$ penalises one that is too low. For one day, the best is $\sigma_t^2=r_t^2$. The model must do it for all days with three numbers.

Why factorise instead of assuming independence?

Returns are not independent, so the joint density is not the product of marginals. It is always the product of conditionals: $f(r_1,r_2)=f(r_2\mid r_1)f(r_1)$.

DIAGNOSTICS Did the model work? Look at $\hat z_t=r_t/\hat\sigma_t$— ⊡ ✕

If the model is right, the GARCH innovations $\hat z_t=r_t/\hat\sigma_t$ should be i.i.d. with mean 0 and variance 1. So:

  • plot of $\hat z_t$: no more volatility clusters (slide 16);
  • ACF of $\hat z_t^2$: flat, while the ACF of $r_t^2$ is strongly positive (slide 17);
  • mean about 0, variance about 1.

What the deck finds (S&P 500, 1980–2020). GARCH volatility tracks the market's variability: peaks in 1987, 2008, 2020 (annualized, often above 50%). Innovations show much less clustering than returns. But some $\hat z_t$ are as low as $-10$: non-normal, with a heavy left tail. The empirical 1% quantile of $\hat z$ is $-2.6$, not $-2.33$.

"GARCH filter"

Dividing by $\hat\sigma_t$ "devolatilizes" the returns. It is the time-series version of standardizing residuals.

?●●practiceMLE when variance is constant

Set $\beta_1=\beta_2=0$. Show that the MLE of $\sigma^2=\beta_0$ is $\frac1T\sum r_t^2$.

Solution

$\log L=-\frac12(T\log2\pi+T\log\sigma^2+\sum r_t^2/\sigma^2)$. F.O.C.: $\frac T{\sigma^2}=\frac{\sum r_t^2}{\sigma^4}\Rightarrow\hat\sigma^2=\frac1T\sum r_t^2$: the sample variance with mean 0. GARCH lets that number move over time.

?●●practiceFilter three days

$\beta=(0.05,0.9,0.05)$, $\sigma_1^2=2$, $r=(0,3,-1)$. Compute $\sigma_2^2,\sigma_3^2,\sigma_4^2$ and $z_3$.

Solution

$\sigma_2^2=0.05+1.8+0=1.85$. $\sigma_3^2=0.05+1.665+0.45=2.165$. $\sigma_4^2=0.05+1.9485+0.05=2.0485$. $z_3=-1/\sqrt{2.165}=-0.68$.
