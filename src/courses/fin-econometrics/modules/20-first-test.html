The first test: one parameter, σ² known

lecture 6 · lecture 7, slides 2–5
READ FIRST Standardize, then look at where you land— ⊡ ✕

With one restriction, $m$ is a number and we can skip the quadratic form: divide $m$ by its standard deviation and compare with $N(0,1)$. A value like 0.1 is perfectly ordinary for a standard normal; a value like 4 is almost impossible. So 4 is evidence against $H_0$.

DEF The statistic— ⊡ ✕

$H_0:\beta_1=0$. $R=[1\ 0\cdots0]$, $q=0$, $m=Rb-q=b_1$.

In general $b_1\mid X\sim N\big(\beta_1,\ \sigma^2R(X'X)^{-1}R'\big)$, where $\sigma^2R(X'X)^{-1}R'$ is just the $[1,1]$ element of $\sigma^2(X'X)^{-1}$: $\mathbb V[b_1\mid X]$.

$$\boxed{z=\frac{b_1}{\sqrt{\sigma^2R(X'X)^{-1}R'}}\ \overset{H_0}{\sim}\ N(0,1)}$$

For a general single restriction: $z=\dfrac{Rb-q}{\sqrt{\sigma^2R(X'X)^{-1}R'}}$.

We assume $\sigma^2$ known for now, so that $z$ can be computed. Lecture 7 replaces it by $s^2$.

KEY Size, critical values, and the slide's open question z = 1.9— ⊡ ✕

Choose the size (also called significance level) of the test, e.g. 5%: the probability of rejecting a true $H_0$. It fixes the critical values:

alternativereject if10%5%1%
$H_A:\beta_1\neq0$ (two-sided)$\lvert z\rvert>c$1.6451.962.576
$H_A:\beta_1>0$ (one-sided)$z>c$1.2821.6452.326

$z=1.9$, size 5%.

  • Against $H_A:\beta_1>0$ (lecture 6's alternative): $1.9>1.645$ ⇒ reject.
  • Against $H_A:\beta_1\neq0$ (lecture 7's alternative): $1.9<1.96$ ⇒ do not reject.
  • At 1%: do not reject in either case.

The same data, two answers. That is why $H_A$ and the size are stated before looking at the data.

Vocabulary

The slides call 5% the "confidence level or size". Elsewhere (and in your statistics course) the confidence level is $1-\alpha=95\%$ and 5% is the size or significance level. If an exam question says "5% confidence level" it means a 5% rejection area, as in W8.

INTERACTIVE Reject or not?— ⊡ ✕

p-value = probability, under $H_0$, of a statistic at least as extreme as the one observed (in the direction(s) of $H_A$). Reject when the p-value is below the size. Try this: set $z=1.9$ and switch between two-sided and right tail. Then pick "t with 3 d.f.": the critical value moves to 3.18.

W10 TUTORIAL · EX 1 · "test if the slope is 3"

Regression of $y$ on a constant and $x_1$: $\hat y=1+0.5x_1$, $n=100$, $\sigma^2=2$ known, $X'X=\begin{bmatrix}3&2\\1&4\end{bmatrix}$, $\alpha=5\%$. Would the result change if $\sigma^2=20$?

  1. Hypotheses. $H_0:\beta_2=3$ vs $H_A:\beta_2\neq3$.
  2. Statistic. $J=1$ and $\sigma^2$ known ⇒ $\dfrac{Rb-q}{\sqrt{\sigma^2R(X'X)^{-1}R'}}\sim N(0,1)$ under $H_0$.
  3. $R=[0\ 1]$, $q=3$. Numerator: $0.5-3=-2.5$.
  4. Denominator. $\det=12-2=10$, $(X'X)^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}0.4&-0.2\\-0.1&0.3\end{bmatrix}$, $R(X'X)^{-1}R'=0.3$, so $\sqrt{2\cdot0.3}=\sqrt{0.6}=0.775$.
  5. Value: $-2.5/0.775=\mathbf{-3.23}$. $\lvert-3.23\rvert>1.96$ ⇒ reject.

With $\sigma^2=20$: $-2.5/\sqrt{20\cdot0.3}=-2.5/2.449=\mathbf{-1.02}$. $1.02<1.96$ ⇒ do not reject. More noise: the same distance from 3 is no longer surprising.

Two remarks on the sheet

(1) The notes call the numerator "$b_1$"; it is $Rb-q=b_2-3$. (2) The given $X'X$ is not symmetric and its $(1,1)$ element is not $n=100$, so no real data produced it. Use it for the mechanics only.

?●practiceOne-sided alpha test

$\hat\alpha=0.05$, $\mathbb V[\hat\alpha\mid X]=0.0009$ known. Test $H_0:\alpha=0$ vs $H_A:\alpha>0$ at 5% and 1%.

Solution

$z=0.05/0.03=1.667$. At 5%: $1.667>1.645$ ⇒ reject (barely). At 1%: $1.667<2.326$ ⇒ do not reject. p-value $=P(Z>1.667)=0.048$.

?●●practiceBeta equal to one?

$\hat\beta=0.74$, $\text{sd}=0.12$ known. Test $H_0:\beta=1$ two-sided at 5%. And $H_0:\beta=0$?

Solution

$z=(0.74-1)/0.12=-2.17$: $\lvert z\rvert>1.96$ ⇒ reject $\beta=1$. For $\beta=0$: $z=6.17$ ⇒ reject. Forgetting to subtract $q$ is a classic mistake: software prints the test of zero.

?●●practiceFrom z to a confidence interval

Find all values $q$ for which $H_0:\beta_2=q$ is not rejected at 5% two-sided, given $b_2=0.74$, sd $0.12$.

Solution

$\lvert0.74-q\rvert\le1.96\cdot0.12=0.235$: $q\in[0.505,\ 0.975]$. That set is the 95% confidence interval. So "0 is outside the CI" and "reject $\beta_2=0$ at 5%" are the same statement.
