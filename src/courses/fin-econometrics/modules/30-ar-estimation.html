Estimating AR models; AR(p) and ARMA(p,q)

time series deck, slides 13–15
READ FIRST It's just a regression with a lagged regressor— ⊡ ✕

An AR(1) is a linear model whose regressor is yesterday's value. So OLS estimates it. AR(p) works the same way with $p$ lags. ARMA does not, because it has past errors on the right-hand side and those are not observed.

KEY AR(1) as $y=X\beta+\varepsilon$— ⊡ ✕

For $t=2,\dots,T$ (we lose the first observation):

$$\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}Y_2\\\vdots\\Y_T\end{bmatrix}}_{y\ ((T-1)\times1)}=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}1&Y_1\\\vdots&\vdots\\1&Y_{T-1}\end{bmatrix}}_{X\ ((T-1)\times2)}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\phi_0\\\phi_1\end{bmatrix}}_{\beta\ (2\times1)}+\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon_2\\\vdots\\\varepsilon_T\end{bmatrix}}_{\varepsilon\ ((T-1)\times1)}$$ $$b=(X'X)^{-1}X'y=\begin{bmatrix}\hat\phi_0\\\hat\phi_1\end{bmatrix},\qquad s^2=\frac{e'e}{(T-1)-2}$$

By module 09: $\hat\phi_1=\dfrac{\widehat{\text{Cov}}[Y_t,Y_{t-1}]}{\hat{\mathbb V}[Y_{t-1}]}$, essentially the first sample autocorrelation. It matches $\rho(1)=\phi_1$.

$s^2$ estimates $\sigma_\varepsilon^2$. The unconditional variance is estimated by $s^2/(1-\hat\phi_1^2)$.

A subtlety beyond the slides

$X$ contains lagged $y$, so $\varepsilon_2$ affects later regressors and $\mathbb E[\varepsilon\mid X]=0$ cannot hold for all rows. OLS is then biased in small samples (towards zero for $\phi_1$ near 1) but consistent, and fine for large $T$ if $\lvert\phi_1\rvert<1$.

INTERACTIVE Sampling distribution of $\hat\phi_1$— ⊡ ✕

Try this. $T=50,\ \phi_1=0.9$: the average estimate is below 0.9 and the histogram is skewed left. Raise $T$ to 500: the bias is nearly gone. At $\phi_1=1$ the histogram stays skewed however large $T$ is: standard $t$-tests don't work for a random walk.

EXTENSIONS AR(p) and ARMA(p,q)— ⊡ ✕

AR(p): $Y_t=\phi_0+\phi_1Y_{t-1}+\dots+\phi_pY_{t-p}+\varepsilon_t$

Slide question: how to estimate? OLS again, for $t=p+1,\dots,T$:

$$\begin{bmatrix}Y_{p+1}\\\vdots\\Y_T\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&Y_p&\cdots&Y_1\\\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\1&Y_{T-1}&\cdots&Y_{T-p}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\phi_0\\\phi_1\\\vdots\\\phi_p\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon_{p+1}\\\vdots\\\varepsilon_T\end{bmatrix}$$

$y$ is $(T-p)\times1$, $X$ is $(T-p)\times(p+1)$, $\beta$ is $(p+1)\times1$.

ARMA(p,q):

$$Y_t=\phi_0+\sum_{i=1}^p\phi_iY_{t-i}+\varepsilon_t+\sum_{j=1}^q\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j}$$

"Estimation more involved because of richer error structure ($\varepsilon$'s not observed)": the columns $\varepsilon_{t-j}$ of the would-be $X$ are unknown, so OLS is not available. One uses maximum likelihood, the same route as GARCH (module 33).

AR(2) mean by the stationarity trick: $\mu=\phi_0+\phi_1\mu+\phi_2\mu\Rightarrow\mu=\frac{\phi_0}{1-\phi_1-\phi_2}$.

EXAMPLE · estimate an AR(1) by hand

$y_1,\dots,y_6=1,\,2,\,2,\,3,\,2,\,2$. Pairs (regressor, dependent): $(1,2),(2,2),(2,3),(3,2),(2,2)$.

$\bar x=2$, $\bar y=2.2$. $S_{xy}=(-1)(-0.2)+0+0+(1)(-0.2)+0=0$. $S_{xx}=2$. $\hat\phi_1=0$, $\hat\phi_0=2.2$. In this tiny sample there is no linear memory: the forecast is 2.2 whatever today's value. Five observations are far too few to conclude anything; the point is the construction of the pairs: 6 observations give 5 rows.

You have 200 monthly observations and estimate an AR(3). Dimensions of $y,X,\beta$? d.f. of the $t$-test for $\phi_3=0$? Write $H_0$: "only the first lag matters" as $R\beta=q$.

Solution

$y$: $197\times1$; $X$: $197\times4$; $\beta=(\phi_0,\phi_1,\phi_2,\phi_3)'$. d.f. $197-4=193$. $R=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&1&0\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}$, $q=(0,0)'$: $F_{2,193}$.

?●●practiceTest for no persistence

$T=101$, $\hat\phi_1=0.3$, se $=0.095$. Test $H_0:\phi_1=0$ at 5%. What does it mean for forecasting?

Solution

100 rows, d.f. 98. $t=3.16>1.98$ ⇒ reject: today's value helps to forecast tomorrow's. If not rejected, the best forecast would be the mean.
