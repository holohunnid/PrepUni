TRAPS Twenty mistakes to avoid— ⊡ ✕
  • Writing $\varepsilon$ for $e$ or $\beta$ for $b$.
  • "$X'e=0$, so $X$ is exogenous."
  • "OLS needs normality." It does not, for anything before lecture 5.
  • $(X'X)^{-1}_{kk}=1/(X'X)_{kk}$.
  • $s^2=e'e/n$, or forgetting that $K$ includes the constant.
  • $R^2$ with non-de-meaned data.
  • Calling a low $R^2$ a bad estimate of $\beta$.
  • Forgetting $q$ in $Rb-q$ (testing $\beta=1$ with the $t$ for 0).
  • Dropping the covariance in $\mathbb V[b_2+b_3]$.
  • Counting $\beta_2=\beta_3=\beta_4$ as three restrictions.
  • Using $t$ when $\sigma^2$ is known, or $F$ when it is.
  • Forgetting to divide by $J$ (or dividing in the $\chi^2$).
  • Swapping the $F$ degrees of freedom.
  • Two-sided critical value for a one-sided test.
  • "Accept $H_0$" as "$H_0$ is proved".
  • "Smaller size, so a better test": Type II rises.
  • $\mathbb E[Y_t]=\phi_0$; conditional variance $=\frac{\sigma^2}{1-\phi_1^2}$.
  • Swapping $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ of the GARCH (deck vs textbooks).
  • VaR with $\sigma_t$ in place of $\sigma_{t+1}$, or with a negative sign.
  • "VaR is the worst loss."
