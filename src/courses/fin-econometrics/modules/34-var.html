Value-at-Risk and backtesting

GARCH deck, slides 18–22
READ FIRST How much can I lose on a bad day?— ⊡ ✕

VaR at 1% over one day is the loss that will be exceeded on only 1 day in 100. Regulators (Basel Committee, 1996) impose it and banks often compute it with GARCH, because then today's VaR depends on today's volatility. The formula is one line once you see that $\sigma_{t+1}$ is known today.

DEF VaR— ⊡ ✕

VaR is (essentially) a left-tail quantile of the profit-and-loss distribution, for a given level and time horizon. At day $t$, level 1%, one day, $r_{t+1}=\log(S_{t+1}/S_t)$:

$$\boxed{0.01=P(r_{t+1}<-VaR_{t,t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t)}$$

VaR is a positive number; minus VaR is the quantile. On slide 19: $0.01=P(r_{t+1}\lt-0.05\mid\mathcal F_t)$, so $VaR=0.05$: "with 99% probability I will not lose more than 5% tomorrow".

Derivation in any $r_{t+1}=\sigma_{t+1}z_{t+1}$ model:

$$\begin{aligned}0.01&=P(r_{t+1}<-VaR_{t,t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t)\\&=P(\sigma_{t+1}z_{t+1}<-VaR_{t,t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t)\\&=P\Big(z_{t+1}<-\frac{VaR_{t,t+1}}{\sigma_{t+1}}\,\Big|\,\mathcal F_t\Big)\end{aligned}$$

so $-VaR_{t,t+1}/\sigma_{t+1}=z_{0.01}$, the 1% quantile of $z$:

$$\boxed{VaR_{t,t+1}=-\sigma_{t+1}\,z_{0.01}}$$

Dividing by $\sigma_{t+1}$ is allowed because $\sigma_{t+1}\in\mathcal F_t$: a known positive number.

INTERACTIVE VaR and ES on the density— ⊡ ✕

Try this. VaR is proportional to $\sigma_{t+1}$: double the volatility, double the VaR. Compare normal and $t_4$ at 1% and at 10%: at 1% heavy tails give a larger VaR; at 10% the $t$ with the same variance gives a smaller one, because more mass sits in the centre.

EXAMPLE · from GARCH to VaR in francs

$\beta=(0.02,0.90,0.08)$, today $\sigma_t^2=1$, $r_t=-3$ (percent), so $\sigma_{t+1}^2=1.64$, $\sigma_{t+1}=1.281\%$ (module 32). Portfolio 1,000,000.

Normal $z$: $VaR=2.326\cdot1.281\%=2.98\%$, about 29,800.

Empirical $z_{0.01}=-2.6$: $3.33\%$, about 33,300.

The day before, with $\sigma_t=1\%$: 23,300. One bad day raised the 1% VaR by 28%. A constant-volatility VaR would not have moved.

BACKTEST Does the VaR have the right number of violations?— ⊡ ✕

A violation on day $t+1$: $r_{t+1}\lt-VaR_{t,t+1}$. If VaR is right, violations occur 1% of the time (by definition of quantile) and are unpredictable.

Is it "close" to 1%? The number of violations is Binomial$(N,0.01)$:

  • 10,303 days: mean 103, sd $\sqrt{N\cdot0.01\cdot0.99}=10.1$. $z=(94-103)/10.1=-0.9$: fine.
  • 258 days: mean 2.58, sd 1.6. $z=1.5$, and $P(\ge5)=0.12$: not unusual even in the Covid year.

A second check: violations should not cluster. A VaR with constant $\sigma$ may have 1% violations overall, all in 2008 and 2020: it fails in crises.
