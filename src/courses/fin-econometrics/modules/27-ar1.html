AR(1) and the Wold representation

time series deck, slides 2–6
READ FIRST A regression of today on yesterday— ⊡ ✕

Much financial data come as time series, and we mostly want forecasts. The simplest useful model says: today's value is a linear function of yesterday's plus a new shock. One parameter, $\phi_1$, decides everything: how long shocks last, whether the series wanders off, what the forecast looks like.

DEF AR(1)— ⊡ ✕
$$\boxed{Y_t=\phi_0+\phi_1Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t,\qquad\varepsilon_t\sim\text{i.i.d. }N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2)}$$

$\phi_0,\phi_1$ constants, $t=1,2,\dots$ The process is "put in motion" by the sequence of i.i.d. shocks.

$\phi_1$behaviour
$0$i.i.d.: no persistence, no memory
$0\lt\phi_1\lt1$persistent: high values tend to be followed by high values, then back to the mean
$-1\lt\phi_1\lt0$zig-zag around the mean
$1$random walk: infinitely persistent, never returns to a mean
$\lvert\phi_1\rvert>1$explosive

KEY The Wold representation: an AR(1) is a sum of all past shocks— ⊡ ✕

Substitute backwards:

$$\begin{aligned}Y_t&=\phi_0+\phi_1Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t\\&=\phi_0+\phi_1(\phi_0+\phi_1Y_{t-2}+\varepsilon_{t-1})+\varepsilon_t\\&=\phi_0+\phi_0\phi_1+\phi_1^2Y_{t-2}+\varepsilon_t+\phi_1\varepsilon_{t-1}\\&=\phi_0(1+\phi_1+\phi_1^2+\dots)+\varepsilon_t+\phi_1\varepsilon_{t-1}+\phi_1^2\varepsilon_{t-2}+\dots\end{aligned}$$ $$\boxed{Y_t=\frac{\phi_0}{1-\phi_1}+\sum_{j=0}^\infty\phi_1^j\,\varepsilon_{t-j}}\qquad\lvert\phi_1\rvert<1$$

Two things happen only if $\lvert\phi_1\rvert<1$: the geometric series $1+\phi_1+\phi_1^2+\dots$ converges to $\frac1{1-\phi_1}$, and $\phi_1^kY_{t-k}\to0$. "Not explosion": this is the stationarity condition.

Reading: $Y_t$ = long-run mean + weighted sum of all shocks ever, with weights $1,\phi_1,\phi_1^2,\dots$ fading geometrically. A unit shock today moves $Y_{t+h}$ by $\phi_1^h$ (impulse response).

Half-life: the $h$ with $\phi_1^h=\frac12$: $h=\dfrac{\ln0.5}{\ln\phi_1}$. For $0.5$: 1 period; for $0.9$: 6.6; for $0.99$: 69.

RESULT Unconditional moments— ⊡ ✕

From the Wold form, with $\mathbb E[\varepsilon_t]=0$ for all $t$:

$$\mathbb E[Y_t]=\frac{\phi_0}{1-\phi_1}$$

With i.i.d. shocks (no covariances), variances add:

$$\mathbb V[Y_t]=\sum_{j=0}^\infty\mathbb V[\phi_1^j\varepsilon_{t-j}]=\sigma_\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=0}^\infty\phi_1^{2j}=\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{1-\phi_1^2}$$

Slide question: the unconditional distribution? A linear combination of independent normals is normal:

$$Y_t\sim N\Big(\frac{\phi_0}{1-\phi_1},\ \frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{1-\phi_1^2}\Big)$$

Shortcut under stationarity. $\mu=\phi_0+\phi_1\mu\Rightarrow\mu=\frac{\phi_0}{1-\phi_1}$. $v=\phi_1^2v+\sigma_\varepsilon^2\Rightarrow v=\frac{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}{1-\phi_1^2}$ ($\varepsilon_t$ is independent of $Y_{t-1}$).

TRAP

$\phi_0$ is not the mean of $Y_t$ (unless $\phi_1=0$).

?●practiceMoments

$Y_t=2+0.6Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t$, $\sigma_\varepsilon^2=4$. Mean, variance, distribution, 95% range of $Y_t$.

Solution

Mean $2/0.4=5$. Variance $4/(1-0.36)=6.25$, sd 2.5. $Y_t\sim N(5,6.25)$. Range $5\pm1.96\cdot2.5=[0.1,\ 9.9]$.

?●●practiceBack out the parameters

A stationary AR(1) has mean 10, variance 25 and $\phi_1=0.8$. Find $\phi_0$ and $\sigma_\varepsilon^2$.

Solution

$\phi_0=10\cdot0.2=2$. $\sigma_\varepsilon^2=25(1-0.64)=9$.

?●●practiceDe-mean it

Show that $\tilde Y_t=Y_t-\mu$ follows an AR(1) with no constant and the same $\phi_1$.

Solution

$Y_t-\mu=\phi_0+\phi_1Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t-\mu$. Since $\mu=\phi_0+\phi_1\mu$, $\phi_0-\mu=-\phi_1\mu$, so $\tilde Y_t=\phi_1(Y_{t-1}-\mu)+\varepsilon_t=\phi_1\tilde Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t$. That is why the ACF slide can set $\phi_0=0$ "for simplicity".
