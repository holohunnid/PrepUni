Statistics — MSc (S. Peluso)

The MSc statistics course, rebuilt lecture by lecture. The handwritten lecture notes come first: every example the lecturer worked on the board is here, solved step by step, with a picture next to it. The PDF decks (the "PDF notes" the lecturer refers to), the lecturer's own notes and the exercise sheets fill in the details and the steps the board skipped. Lectures 1–5 are the probability block, up to the normal, χ² and Student-t. Lectures 5–9 turn the direction around: from data back to the model, with point estimation (sampling distributions, method of moments, maximum likelihood, MSE, consistency) and confidence intervals.

Every module opens with a READ FIRST panel that says in plain words what it is about. Then intuition, then the formula, then why the formula is stated the way it is. The figures with sliders are there to be played with — each one has preset buttons that reproduce the lecture's own numbers.

How to use: 01–09 = lecture 1 (sets, axioms, conditioning, independence). 10–15 = lecture 2 (Bayes, Monty Hall, random variables, the CDF, transformations, the pmf). 16–21 = lecture 3 (expectation, variance, then the named discrete distributions — start with module 18, a map of the whole family). 22–26 = lectures 4–5 (continuous random variables, uniform, exponential, normal, χ², Student-t, quantiles and VaR). 27–30 = lectures 5–6 (populations, samples, sample statistics, sampling distributions of $\bar X$ and $S^2$). 31–34 = lectures 6–7 (analogy, method of moments, maximum likelihood — the exam's open question). 35–36 = lectures 7–8 (MSE, bias, efficiency, consistency). 37–38 = lectures 8–9 (confidence intervals). 39 = exam-style multiple choice (theory and R) plus an index of every example with its answer. The R corners throughout are there because a third of the exam is multiple choice on R.
