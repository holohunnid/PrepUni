03

Inclusion–exclusion & the power set

lecture 1, notes p.3–4 · slides: Axioms 4–8
READ FIRST Counting a union without double counting— ⊡ ✕

Write $|A|$ for the cardinality of $A$: the number of outcomes $\omega\in A$. How many outcomes are in $A\cup B$? The tempting answer $|A|+|B|$ is wrong whenever the two sets overlap, because every outcome in $A\cap B$ gets counted twice — once as a member of $A$, once as a member of $B$. The fix is to subtract the overlap once. That is the whole inclusion–exclusion principle: include the pieces, exclude what you counted too often, and repeat.

Why do we care in a probability course? Because in module 05 exactly the same formula holds with $P$ in place of $|\cdot|$: $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)$. Probability behaves like a (weighted) way of counting, and this module is where the pattern is easiest to see.

RULE Two sets— ⊡ ✕
$$\boxed{\;|A\cup B|=|A|+|B|-|A\cap B|\;}$$
EXAMPLE · one die
$A=\{2,4,6\}$, $B=\{1,2\}$, $A\cap B=\{2\}$: $$|A\cup B|=3+2-1=4\qquad\text{and indeed }A\cup B=\{1,2,4,6\}.$$
RULE Three sets— ⊡ ✕
$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}|A\cup B\cup C|=\;&|A|+|B|+|C|\\&-|A\cap B|-|B\cap C|-|A\cap C|\\&+|A\cap B\cap C|\end{aligned}}$$

Add the three sets, subtract the three pairwise overlaps, add back the triple overlap. The pattern generalises to $n$ sets with alternating signs: singles $+$, pairs $-$, triples $+$, quadruples $-$, …

Why the $+$ at the end? Step through it on the right: after adding the three sets the centre is counted 3 times; subtracting the three pairwise overlaps removes it 3 times — now it is counted 0 times, so it has to be put back once.

EXAMPLE · the notes' three die events
$E_1=\{2,4,6\}$, $E_2=\{2,3,5\}$, $E_3=\{6\}$. Overlaps: $E_1\cap E_2=\{2\}$, $E_2\cap E_3=\emptyset$, $E_1\cap E_3=\{6\}$, triple $=\emptyset$. $$|E_1\cup E_2\cup E_3|=3+3+1-1-0-1+0=5$$ Check: $E_1\cup E_2\cup E_3=\{2,3,4,5,6\}$ — five faces (only 1 is missing) ✓.

Finance flavour
A bank has 100 clients: 60 hold stocks, 50 hold bonds, 30 hold both. Clients holding at least one: $60+50-30=80$; holding neither: $100-80=20$. Divide everything by 100 and you are already doing probability: $P(S\cup B)=0.6+0.5-0.3=0.8$.
DEF The power set: the list of all possible events— ⊡ ✕

The power set $\mathcal P(\Omega)$ is the set of all subsets of $\Omega$ — i.e. the complete list of events you could ever ask about.

Coin (notes p.3–4): $\Omega=\{H,T\}$ and

$$\mathcal P(\Omega)=\big\{\{\ \},\{H\},\{T\},\{H,T\}\big\},\qquad|\mathcal P(\Omega)|=2^{|\Omega|}=2^2=4.$$

Die: $\Omega=\{1,\dots,6\}$ and

$$\mathcal P(\Omega)=\big\{\{\ \},\{1\},\dots,\{6\},\{1,2\},\dots,\{5,6\},\{1,2,3\},\dots,\Omega\big\},\quad|\mathcal P(\Omega)|=2^6=64.$$

Why $2^{|\Omega|}$?

To build a subset, walk through the outcomes one by one and decide in or out for each. That is $|\Omega|$ independent yes/no decisions, so $2\times2\times\cdots\times2=2^{|\Omega|}$ different subsets. The tree on the right does it for the coin.

Where this is going
Kolmogorov's axioms (module 04) assign a probability $P(E)$ to each element of $\mathcal P(\Omega)$. For finite $\Omega$ you never need to list them all — the rules in module 05 let you compute any $P(E)$ from a few basic numbers.

?check yourselfCounting events
$\Omega=\{a,b,c\}$. How many events contain the outcome $a$?
Answer
$a$ is forced "in"; $b$ and $c$ are free: $2^2=4$ events — $\{a\},\{a,b\},\{a,c\},\{a,b,c\}$. Exactly half of the $2^3=8$ events.
