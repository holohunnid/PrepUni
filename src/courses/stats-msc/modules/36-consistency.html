Consistency & MLE asymptotics

lecture 8, pp.1–5 of the lecture notes · Point Estimation deck §8–9
READ FIRST What happens when the sample grows without limit— ⊡ ✕

Module 35 judged estimators at a fixed sample size ("finite-sample performance"). Sometimes estimators are compared by pretending that $n\to+\infty$: properties that hold "asymptotically". The minimum we should ask of any estimator: with more and more data, it should home in on the truth. That is consistency.

We no longer look at one estimator but at a sequence $\{T_n=t_n(X_1,\dots,X_n)\}$: the same recipe applied to samples of size $1,2,3,\dots$ The lecture defines three properties, then states the four big asymptotic properties of the MLE. Those explain why the MLE is the default estimator in practice, and they lead straight into confidence intervals (modules 37–38).

What you need for the exam
The three definitions and how they relate (strong ⇒ weak; strong ⇔ bias → 0 and variance → 0). Checking strong consistency by letting $n\to\infty$ in an MSE formula. The four MLE properties, and $\hat\theta\approx N\big(\theta,I(\theta)^{-1}\big)$.
LECTURE 8 Three asymptotic properties (lecture 8, p.1)— ⊡ ✕

1) Weak consistency. $\{T_n\}$ is (weakly) consistent if, $\forall\theta\in\Theta$,

$$\boxed{\;\lim_{n\to+\infty}P\big(|T_n-\theta|<\varepsilon\big)=1\quad\forall\varepsilon>0\;}$$

As we collect more data, $T_n$ and $\theta$ get closer and closer. Also known as consistency in probability. Equivalently $P(|T_n-\theta|\ge\varepsilon)\to0$.

2) Asymptotic unbiasedness. $\{T_n\}$ is asymptotically unbiased if, $\forall\theta\in\Theta$,

$$\boxed{\;\lim_{n\to+\infty}B_\theta(T_n)=0\;},\qquad B_\theta(T_n)=\mathbb E(T_n)-\theta.$$

With $n$ sufficiently high, there is no distortion.

3) Strong consistency (consistency in MSE). $\{T_n\}$ is strongly consistent for $\theta$ if, $\forall\theta\in\Theta$,

$$\boxed{\;\lim_{n\to+\infty}\text{MSE}_\theta(T_n)=0\;}$$

Note 1: strong consistency ⇒ weak consistency.

Note 2: thanks to $\text{MSE}_\theta(T_n)=\mathbb V(T_n)+B_\theta(T_n)^2$ (module 35), and since both terms are $\ge0$, strong consistency is equivalent to

$$\begin{cases}\lim_{n\to\infty}B_\theta(T_n)=0&\text{(asymptotically unbiased)}\\ \lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb V(T_n)=0&\text{(vanishing variance)}\end{cases}$$

Often it is more convenient to check strong consistency (MSE → 0, a limit of an ordinary formula) than weak consistency (a limit of probabilities). Strong then implies weak.

LECTURE 8 · p.2 · deck p.58 · $\bar X_n$ is weakly consistent for μ when X ~ N(μ, 1)

$\bar X_n=\frac1n\sum X_i\sim N\big(\mu,\frac1n\big)$ (module 30).

  1. $P(|\bar X_n-\mu|<\varepsilon)=P(-\varepsilon<\bar X_n-\mu<\varepsilon)=P(\bar X_n-\mu<\varepsilon)-P(\bar X_n-\mu<-\varepsilon)$.
  2. Standardise, dividing by $\sqrt{1/n}$: $\frac{\bar X_n-\mu}{\sqrt{1/n}}\sim N(0,1)$, so $$=\Phi\Big(\frac\varepsilon{\sqrt{1/n}}\Big)-\Phi\Big(-\frac\varepsilon{\sqrt{1/n}}\Big)$$ ($\Phi$ = cdf of $N(0,1)$, pnorm in R).
  3. Symmetry, $\Phi(-a)=1-\Phi(a)$: $=2\Phi\big(\frac\varepsilon{\sqrt{1/n}}\big)-1=2\Phi(\varepsilon\sqrt n)-1$.
  4. $n\to+\infty$: $\varepsilon\sqrt n\to+\infty$, $\Phi(+\infty)=1$, so the limit is $2\cdot1-1=1$.

⇒ $\bar X_n$ is weakly consistent for $\mu$ when $X\sim N(\mu,1)$.

LECTURE 8 · p.3 · deck p.59 · $\hat\sigma^2$ is biased, yet strongly consistent

$X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, $\hat\sigma^2=\frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2$. From previous examples (module 35):

$$B_{\sigma^2}(\hat\sigma^2)=-\frac1n\sigma^2,\qquad\text{MSE}_{\sigma^2}(\hat\sigma^2)=\frac{2n-1}{n^2}\sigma^4.$$
  1. $\hat\sigma^2$ is biased downwards, but asymptotically unbiased, since $-\frac1n\sigma^2\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0$.
  2. Also $\frac{2n-1}{n^2}\sigma^4\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0$ (the denominator grows like $n^2$, the numerator like $n$).
  3. ⇒ $\hat\sigma^2$ is strongly consistent for $\sigma^2$, and then also weakly consistent.

The same check for the others: $\text{MSE}(S^2)=\frac{2\sigma^4}{n-1}\to0$, and $\text{MSE}(\bar X)=\frac{\sigma^2}n\to0$, so both are strongly consistent. The constant $T_2=40$ of module 35 is not consistent: its MSE $(\sigma^2-40)^2$ does not depend on $n$ at all. Exercise set 2, Q2(e) asked the asymptotic-unbiasedness question for $\hat\sigma^2$ (module 31).

TRAP · unbiased is not consistent, consistent is not unbiased
$\hat\sigma^2$ is biased and consistent. The estimator "$X_1$" (use only the first observation) is unbiased for $\mu$ but not consistent: its variance stays $\sigma^2$ whatever $n$ is. The two properties are about different things.
THEOREM Asymptotic properties of the MLE (lecture 8, pp.3–4 · deck §9)— ⊡ ✕

If $\hat\theta$ is the MLE of $\theta$ then (under regularity conditions):

  1. $\{\hat\theta_n\}$ is strongly consistent for $\theta$;
  2. $\{\hat\theta_n\}$ is asymptotically unbiased, though we may have a finite-sample bias ($\hat\sigma^2$!);
  3. $\{\hat\theta_n\}$ is asymptotically efficient, with minimum variance equal to $I(\theta)^{-1}$, the inverse of the expected Fisher information: $$\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb V(\hat\theta_n)}{1/I(\theta)}=1;$$
  4. $\{\hat\theta_n\}$ converges to a normal RV: $$\lim_{n\to\infty}P\Big(\frac{\hat\theta_n-\theta}{\sqrt{1/I(\theta)}}\le z\Big)=\Phi(z),\quad\text{i.e.}\quad\boxed{\hat\theta_n\ \overset{\text{approx.}}\sim\ N\big(\theta,\ I(\theta)^{-1}\big)}$$

Here $I(\theta)=-\mathbb E\Big[\dfrac{\partial^2}{\partial\theta^2}\ell(\theta;\underline X)\Big]$: the expected curvature of the log-likelihood. A sharply peaked $\ell$ means a lot of information, so a small variance.

Why "efficient" (deck)
The Cramér–Rao inequality says no unbiased estimator can have variance below $1/I(\theta)$. The MLE reaches that floor as $n\to\infty$, so for large samples nothing beats it. For large samples its standard error can be approximated by $\sqrt{1/I(\hat\theta)}$.
Why it matters next
Point 4 is a ready-made pivotal quantity: $\frac{\hat\theta-\theta}{\sqrt{1/I(\hat\theta)}}\approx N(0,1)$. Module 37 builds confidence intervals from exactly this kind of statement: estimator minus parameter, over its standard deviation.

Multi-parameter version (deck): $\hat\theta-\theta$ is approximately multivariate normal with mean $\mathbf 0$ and variance–covariance matrix $I(\hat\theta)^{-1}$.

LECTURE 8 · pp.4–5 · deck pp.60–61 · the Fisher information for σ², and the approximate distribution of $\hat\sigma^2$

$X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, $\{\hat\sigma^2_n\}$ the MLE. Find $I(\sigma^2)=-\mathbb E\Big[\frac{\partial^2}{\partial(\sigma^2)^2}\ell(\theta;\underline X)\Big]$.

  1. $\ln f(x;\mu,\sigma^2)=-\frac12\ln(2\pi\sigma^2)-\frac1{2\sigma^2}(x-\mu)^2$.
  2. $\ell(\sigma^2;\underline x)=-\frac n2\ln(2\pi\sigma^2)-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\sum(x_i-\mu)^2$. To keep the derivatives readable, write $\eta:=\sigma^2$: $$\ell(\eta)=-\frac n2\ln(2\pi\eta)-\frac1{2\eta}\sum(x_i-\mu)^2$$
  3. $\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\eta}=-\frac n{2\eta}+\frac1{2\eta^2}\sum(x_i-\mu)^2$.
  4. $\frac{\partial^2\ell}{\partial\eta^2}=+\frac n{2\eta^2}-\frac1{\eta^3}\sum(x_i-\mu)^2$.
  1. Expectation, using $\mathbb E[(X_i-\mu)^2]=\eta$: $$\mathbb E\Big[\frac{\partial^2\ell}{\partial\eta^2}\Big]=\frac n{2\eta^2}-\frac1{\eta^3}\sum_{i=1}^n\underbrace{\mathbb E[(X_i-\mu)^2]}_{\eta}=\frac n{2\eta^2}-\frac{n\eta}{\eta^3}=\frac n{2\eta^2}-\frac n{\eta^2}=-\frac n{2\eta^2}$$
  2. $I(\sigma^2)=-\mathbb E[\dots]=\boxed{\dfrac n{2\sigma^4}}$, so $\mathbb V(\hat\sigma^2)\approx I(\sigma^2)^{-1}=\dfrac{2\sigma^4}n$.
  3. Then $\boxed{\hat\sigma^2\ \overset{\text{approx.}}\sim\ N\Big(\sigma^2,\ \frac{2\sigma^4}n\Big)}$, further approximated by $N\big(\sigma^2,\frac{2\hat\sigma^4}n\big)$, plugging in the estimate since $\sigma^4$ is unknown.

Check against the exact variance $\mathbb V(\hat\sigma^2)=\frac{2(n-1)}{n^2}\sigma^4$ (module 35): $\frac{2(n-1)\sigma^4/n^2}{2\sigma^4/n}=\frac{n-1}n\to1$. Asymptotically efficient ✓.

?check yourselfWhich are consistent?
For $\mu$: (a) $\bar X_n$; (b) $\frac{X_1+X_n}2$; (c) $\frac1{n+1}\sum X_i$; (d) $\bar X_n+\frac5n$.
Answer
(a) yes. (b) no: variance $\frac{\sigma^2}2$ for every $n$. (c) yes: bias $-\frac\mu{n+1}\to0$, variance $\frac{n\sigma^2}{(n+1)^2}\to0$. (d) yes: bias $\frac5n\to0$, variance $\frac{\sigma^2}n\to0$. (c) and (d) are biased but consistent.
?check yourselfFisher information, Poisson
$X\sim\text{Pois}(\lambda)$. Find $I(\lambda)$ and the approximate distribution of $\hat\lambda=\bar X$.
Answer
$\ell''(\lambda)=-\frac{\sum X_i}{\lambda^2}$, so $I(\lambda)=\frac{\mathbb E\sum X_i}{\lambda^2}=\frac{n\lambda}{\lambda^2}=\frac n\lambda$. Hence $\hat\lambda\approx N\big(\lambda,\frac\lambda n\big)$. Here the variance $\frac\lambda n$ is exact (module 29: $\sigma^2/n$ with $\sigma^2=\lambda$).
