MLE: Poisson, exponential, normal & more

lecture 7, notes pp.51–54 · Point Estimation deck §5.3–5.7 · exercise sets 2–3
READ FIRST The recipe of module 33, applied until it is automatic— ⊡ ✕

Every MLE exercise has the same five lines: $\ln f(x;\theta)$ → $\ell(\theta)=\sum\ln f(x_i;\theta)$ → $\ell'(\theta)=0$ → $\ell''(\hat\theta)<0$ → limits at the boundaries of $\Theta$. This module runs them for the models of the lectures, then covers what happens beyond:

LECTURE 7 · notes p.51 · deck pp.42–43 · X ~ Pois(λ)

$f(x;\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^xe^{-\lambda}}{x!}$, $x=0,1,2,\dots$, $\Theta=(0,\infty)$.

  1. Log of one pmf. $\ln f=\ln\lambda^x+\ln e^{-\lambda}+\ln(x!)^{-1}=x\ln\lambda-\lambda-\ln x!$.
    The last term does not contain $\lambda$: it is useless for finding $\hat\lambda$. Drop it and write "$\propto$" (proportional to, having removed the irrelevant terms): $\ln f\propto x\ln\lambda-\lambda$.
  2. Log-likelihood. $\ell(\lambda)\propto\sum(x_i\ln\lambda-\lambda)=\sum x_i\ln\lambda-n\lambda$.
  3. FOC. $\ell'(\lambda)=\frac{\sum x_i}\lambda-n\overset{\text{fix}}=0\Rightarrow\frac{n\bar x}{\hat\lambda}=n\Rightarrow\boxed{\hat\lambda=\bar x}$, the MLE of $\lambda$ for the Poisson model.
  4. SOC. $\ell''(\lambda)=-\frac{\sum x_i}{\lambda^2}<0$ ⇒ $\hat\lambda$ is a max point (when $\sum x_i>0$).
  5. Boundaries. $\lambda\to0$: $\sum x_i\ln\lambda\to-\infty$ and $n\lambda\to0$, so $\ell\to-\infty$. $\lambda\to\infty$: $\ell=\lambda\big[\sum x_i\frac{\ln\lambda}\lambda-n\big]$, and $\frac{\ln\lambda}\lambda\to0$, so the bracket tends to $-n$ and $\ell\to-\infty$.

For $(2,4,3,3)$: $\hat\lambda=3$, and indeed $L(3)=0.00189>L(3.5)=0.00163$ (module 27).

LECTURE 7 · notes pp.51–52 · deck pp.43–44 · X ~ Exp(λ)

$f(x;\lambda)=\lambda e^{-\lambda x}$, $x>0$, $\Theta=(0,\infty)$.

  1. $\ln f(x;\lambda)=\ln\lambda-\lambda x$.
  2. $\ell(\lambda)=\sum(\ln\lambda-\lambda x_i)=n\ln\lambda-\lambda\sum x_i$.
  3. FOC. $\ell'(\lambda)=\frac n\lambda-\sum x_i\overset{\text{fix}}=0\Rightarrow\frac n{\hat\lambda}=\sum x_i\Rightarrow\boxed{\hat\lambda=\frac n{\sum x_i}=\bar X^{-1}}$
  4. SOC. $\ell''(\lambda)=-\frac n{\lambda^2}<0$ ⇒ max point.
  5. Boundaries. $\lambda\to0$: $n\ln\lambda\to-\infty$, $\lambda\sum x_i\to0$, so $\ell\to-\infty$. $\lambda\to\infty$: $\ell=\lambda\big[n\frac{\ln\lambda}\lambda-\sum x_i\big]$; the bracket tends to the negative constant $-\sum x_i$, so $\ell\to-\infty$.

Same answer as the method of moments (module 32): $1/\bar X$.

LECTURE 7 · notes p.52 · deck pp.44–46 · "a last example on MLE": X ~ N(μ, 1)

$f(x;\mu)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\{-\frac12(x-\mu)^2\}$, $\Theta=\mathbb R$.

  1. $\ln f(x;\mu)=\ln\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}+\ln\exp\{-\tfrac12(x-\mu)^2\}\propto-\frac12(x-\mu)^2$.
  2. $\ell(\mu)\propto\sum-\frac12(x_i-\mu)^2=-\frac12\sum(x_i-\mu)^2$.
  3. FOC. $\ell'(\mu)=-\frac12\sum\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}(x_i-\mu)^2=-\frac12\sum2(x_i-\mu)(-1)=\sum(x_i-\mu)\overset{\text{fix}}=0$.
    $\sum(x_i-\hat\mu)=0\Rightarrow\sum x_i-n\hat\mu=0\Rightarrow\boxed{\hat\mu=\bar x}$
  4. SOC. $\ell''(\mu)=\frac\partial{\partial\mu}\big(\sum x_i-n\mu\big)=-n<0$.
  5. Boundaries. $\lim_{\mu\to\pm\infty}\big[-\frac12\sum(x_i-\mu)^2\big]=-\infty$. ⇒ $\bar X$ is the MLE of $\mu$ when $X\sim N(\mu,1)$.

Deck, with numbers: $\underline x=(1.9,1.2,1.8,0.5)$ gives $\hat\mu=\frac{5.4}4=\mathbf{1.35}$. The plot of $L(\mu)$ shows $L(1)>L(2)$: more evidence for $\mu=1$ than for $\mu=2$, and the peak is at 1.35. The interactive figure of module 33 reproduces it.

Second proof without calculus (deck p.46). Maximising $\ell$ means minimising $g(\mu)=\sum(x_i-\mu)^2$. Add and subtract $\bar x$:

$$g(\mu)=\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2+n(\bar x-\mu)^2+2(\bar x-\mu)\underbrace{\textstyle\sum(x_i-\bar x)}_{0}$$

The first term does not depend on $\mu$, and the second is $\ge0$ and zero exactly at $\mu=\bar x$. So $\hat\mu=\bar x$, the least-squares point.

THEORY Multi-parameter extension (notes pp.52–53 · deck §5.4)— ⊡ ✕

$\theta=(\theta_1,\theta_2,\dots,\theta_k)$, $k>1$. Then

  1. set every partial derivative to zero: $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta_j}\ell(\theta;\underline x)\overset{\text{fix}}=0$, $j=1,\dots,k$ ($k$ equations, $k$ unknowns);
  2. check that the matrix of second derivatives, the Hessian, is negative definite at the solution: $$H=\Big(\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\theta_i\,\partial\theta_j}\ell(\theta;\underline x)\Big)_{i,j}$$ (all eigenvalues negative; for a diagonal $H$, all diagonal entries negative);
  3. check the limits of $\ell(\theta;\underline x)$ at the boundaries of each $\theta_j$.
EXAM
Multi-parameter MLE: theory questions, no exercises to solve. Know the three steps and the result for $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$.

Example: $X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, $\theta=(\mu,\sigma^2)$, $k=2$ (steps omitted in the lecture; here from the deck):

$$\ell(\mu,\sigma^2)=-\frac n2\ln(2\pi\sigma^2)-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\sum(x_i-\mu)^2$$ $$\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial\ell}{\partial\mu}=\dfrac1{\sigma^2}\sum(x_i-\mu)=0\\[8pt]\dfrac{\partial\ell}{\partial\sigma^2}=-\dfrac n{2\sigma^2}+\dfrac1{2\sigma^4}\sum(x_i-\mu)^2=0\end{cases}$$ $$\Rightarrow\ \boxed{\hat\mu=\bar x,\qquad\hat\sigma^2=\frac1n\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2=\frac{n-1}nS^2}$$

At the solution the Hessian is diagonal, $H=\text{diag}\big(-\frac n{\hat\sigma^2},\,-\frac n{2\hat\sigma^4}\big)$, so it is negative definite ✓. Note that $\hat\sigma^2$ is the same estimator as analogy (31) and moments (32): three methods, one answer, and module 35 asks whether it is any good.

THEORY When there is no formula: numerical maximisation (notes p.53 · deck §5.5)— ⊡ ✕

Many times it is not possible to find an analytical solution to the MLE. Then we use numerical procedures to maximise $\ell(\theta;\underline x)$, starting at a guess $\theta^{(0)}$ and updating:

Newton–Raphson

$$\theta^{(t+1)}=\theta^{(t)}+J\big(\theta^{(t)}\big)^{-1}s\big(\theta^{(t)}\big)$$
  • $s(\theta)$ = score function: the vector of first derivatives of $\ell(\theta;\underline x)$;
  • $J(\theta)$ = observed information matrix: the matrix of second derivatives of $\ell$, multiplied by $-1$.

Fisher scoring

$$\theta^{(t+1)}=\theta^{(t)}+I\big(\theta^{(t)}\big)^{-1}s\big(\theta^{(t)}\big),\qquad I(\theta)=\mathbb E\big[J(\theta)\big]$$

$I(\theta)$ is the expected information matrix (the Fisher information). Module 36 uses it for the variance of the MLE.

Limitation (notes p.53)
There is no guarantee that we reach a global max: the algorithm can get stuck at local max points. To avoid it, run it more times with different starting points. If all runs give the same max point, that is further evidence of a global max.
R corner (standard R, not lecture material)
optim(par = start, fn = function(th) -loglik(th, x)) minimises, so pass $-\ell$. Rerun with several par values, as the lecture recommends. One-parameter case: optimize(loglik, c(lo, hi), maximum = TRUE, x = x).

Lecture 8 also wrote on the board the two-parameter density $f(x;\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\beta x^{\alpha-1}(1-x^\alpha)^{\beta-1}$ on $[0,1]$, $\alpha,\beta>0$, which reduces to $U(0,1)$ when $\alpha=\beta=1$. Its likelihood equations have no closed form, so this is a case for the numerical route.

THEORY When the support depends on θ: $U(0,\theta)$ (deck §5.6)— ⊡ ✕

$f(x;\theta)=\frac1\theta$ on $[0,\theta]$. The derivative recipe fails here: $L$ is not differentiable at its maximum. Look at $L$ directly:

$$L(\theta)=\prod_{i=1}^n\frac1\theta\,\mathbb 1\{x_i\le\theta\}=\begin{cases}\theta^{-n}&\theta\ge x_{(n)}=\max x_i\\0&\theta<x_{(n)}\end{cases}$$

If $\theta$ is below the largest observation, that observation was impossible, so $L=0$. Above it, $\theta^{-n}$ is decreasing. The maximum is at the jump: $\boxed{\hat\theta=X_{(n)}=\max\{X_1,\dots,X_n\}}$.

PROPERTY Invariance of the MLE (notes p.54 · deck §5.7)— ⊡ ✕

A nice property of the MLE: the MLE of $\tau(\theta)$, for any function $\tau$, is $\tau(\hat\theta)$:

$$\boxed{\;\widehat{\tau(\theta)}=\tau(\hat\theta)\;}$$

Useful in practice, since we can use $\hat\theta$ without redoing the optimisation to estimate $\tau(\theta)$. It holds even when $\tau$ is not one-to-one. Compare module 29: unbiasedness does not survive $\tau$ ($S^2$ is unbiased, $S$ is not).

  1. Ex 1. $X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, with $\hat\mu=\bar X$ and $\hat\sigma^2=\frac{n-1}nS^2$. MLE of $\sigma$: $\hat\sigma=\sqrt{\hat\sigma^2}=\sqrt{\frac{n-1}n}\,S$. MLE of $\mu^2$: $\bar X^2$.
  2. Ex 2. $X\sim\text{Pois}(\lambda)$, $\hat\lambda=\bar X$. What is the MLE of $P(X=0)$? First $P(X=0)=f(0;\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^0e^{-\lambda}}{0!}=e^{-\lambda}$, a function of $\lambda$. By invariance $\widehat{P(X=0)}=f(0;\hat\lambda)=\boxed{e^{-\bar X}}$.

Deck, also: the MLE depends on the sample only through a sufficient statistic (factorisation theorem): $\sum x_i$ for Bernoulli/Poisson/exponential, $(\bar X,S^2)$ for the normal. Two samples with the same $\sum x_i$ give the same Poisson MLE.

EXERCISES From the exercise sheets (sets 2 and 3)— ⊡ ✕
SET 3 · Q1 · Gamma(α, β), α known: MLE of the scale β

$f(x;\alpha,\beta)=\frac1{\Gamma(\alpha)\beta^\alpha}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-x/\beta}$, $\mathbb E(X)=\alpha\beta$.

  1. $\ln f\propto-\alpha\ln\beta-\frac x\beta$ (terms without $\beta$ dropped).
  2. $\ell(\beta)\propto-n\alpha\ln\beta-\frac{\sum x_i}\beta$.
  3. $\ell'(\beta)=-\frac{n\alpha}\beta+\frac{\sum x_i}{\beta^2}=0\Rightarrow\boxed{\hat\beta=\frac{\bar X}\alpha}$ (answer A).
  4. SOC at $\hat\beta$: $\ell''=\frac{n\alpha}{\beta^2}-\frac{2\sum x_i}{\beta^3}=\frac{n\alpha}{\hat\beta^2}-\frac{2n\alpha}{\hat\beta^2}=-\frac{n\alpha}{\hat\beta^2}<0$ ✓.
  5. (b) Unbiased: $\mathbb E(\hat\beta)=\frac{\mathbb E(\bar X)}\alpha=\frac{\alpha\beta}\alpha=\beta$ ✓.
SET 3 · Q2 · Beta(θ, 1): log-likelihood and MLE

$f(x;\theta)=\theta x^{\theta-1}$, $x\in[0,1]$, $\theta>0$.

  1. (a) $\ell(\theta)=\sum[\ln\theta+(\theta-1)\ln x_i]=n\ln\theta+(\theta-1)\sum\ln x_i$.
  2. (b) $\ell'(\theta)=\frac n\theta+\sum\ln x_i=0\Rightarrow\boxed{\hat\theta=-\frac n{\sum\ln X_i}}$. Positive, because every $\ln x_i<0$. SOC: $-\frac n{\theta^2}<0$ ✓.
  3. (c) MME (module 32): $\tilde\theta=\frac{\bar X}{1-\bar X}$, a different estimator.
SET 3 · Q3 · N(μ, 1) with μ ≥ 0: the boundary matters

Without the constraint $\hat\mu=\bar x$. With $\Theta=[0,\infty)$: $\ell(\mu)\propto-\frac n2(\bar x-\mu)^2$ is a parabola peaked at $\bar x$.

  1. (a) $\underline x=(-0.5,3,5,1,-1)$: $\bar x=7.5/5=1.5\ge0$, inside $\Theta$, so $\hat\mu=\mathbf{1.5}$.
  2. (b) $\underline x=(-3,0,1,-2,-1)$: $\bar x=-1<0$, outside $\Theta$. On $[0,\infty)$ the parabola is decreasing, so the maximum is at the boundary: $\hat\mu=\mathbf 0$.
  3. (c) In general $\boxed{\hat\mu=\max(0,\bar X)}$.
SET 2 · Q5 · $f(k;\alpha)=c\,(1/2)^{\alpha k}$, $k=1,2,\dots$
  1. (a) Constant. $\sum_{k\ge1}(2^{-\alpha})^k=\frac{2^{-\alpha}}{1-2^{-\alpha}}=\frac1{2^\alpha-1}$, so $c=2^\alpha-1$.
  2. (b) MLE. $\ell(\alpha)=n\ln(2^\alpha-1)-\alpha\ln2\sum x_i$. Using $\frac{d}{d\alpha}2^\alpha=\ln2\cdot2^\alpha$: $$\ell'=\frac{n\ln2\cdot2^\alpha}{2^\alpha-1}-\ln2\sum x_i=0\Rightarrow\frac{2^{\hat\alpha}}{2^{\hat\alpha}-1}=\bar x\Rightarrow2^{\hat\alpha}=\frac{\bar x}{\bar x-1}$$ $$\boxed{\hat\alpha=\log_2\frac{\bar X}{\bar X-1}}\ \text{(answer A).}$$ Since $\bar X>1$, $\hat\alpha>0$ ✓.
?check yourselfInvariance, exponential
$X\sim\text{Exp}(\lambda)$ with observed $\bar x=4$. MLE of the mean $\mathbb E(X)$, of the median $\frac{\ln2}\lambda$, and of $P(X>10)$?
Answer
$\hat\lambda=1/4$. Mean $1/\hat\lambda=4$. Median $4\ln2\approx2.77$. $P(X>10)=e^{-10\lambda}$, so its MLE is $e^{-10/4}=e^{-2.5}\approx0.082$.
?check yourselfGeometric
$f(x;p)=(1-p)^{x-1}p$, $x=1,2,\dots$. Find the MLE of $p$.
Answer
$\ell=\sum[(x_i-1)\ln(1-p)+\ln p]=(n\bar x-n)\ln(1-p)+n\ln p$. $\ell'=-\frac{n(\bar x-1)}{1-p}+\frac np=0\Rightarrow1-p=p(\bar x-1)\Rightarrow\hat p=1/\bar X$. The same as the MME.
