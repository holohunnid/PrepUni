HOW TO USE Practise in the exam's own format

The exam is 1.5 hours, closed book: one open question (5 points), five multiple-choice questions on theory (5 points) and five multiple-choice questions on R (5 points). This module mirrors that: click an option — the first click counts — and the explanation appears. The last panel indexes every example of modules 01–25 with its answer and where it is solved.

These questions are written for practice from the course material; they are not past exam questions. The R questions use standard R functions (the R corners in modules 15–25 introduce them).

PART A Multiple choice — theory— ⊡ ✕
A1$A$ and $B$ are independent with $P(A)=0.5$ and $P(B)=0.4$. Then $P(A\cup B)$ equals
Addition rule with the product for the overlap: $0.5+0.4-0.5\cdot0.4=0.7$. "0.9" forgets the overlap (modules 05, 09).
A2$A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive, $P(A)=0.3$, $P(B)=0.4$. Which statement is true?
Disjoint ⇒ $P(A\cap B)=0$ ⇒ $P(A\mid B)=0/0.4=0$. They are not independent ($0\neq0.12$), and $P(A\cup B)=0.7$ (module 09 trap).
A3An event $E$ has $P(E)=0$. Then
$P(E)=0\not\Rightarrow E=\emptyset$: a single point drawn from $(0,1)$ has probability 0 but is not empty (modules 04, 06). $P(E^c)=1$ always follows from the complement rule.
A4$P(D)=0.01$, $P(+\mid D)=0.9$, $P(+\mid D^c)=0.1$. Then $P(D\mid +)$ is closest to
Bayes: $\frac{0.9\cdot0.01}{0.9\cdot0.01+0.1\cdot0.99}=\frac{0.009}{0.108}\approx0.083$. The base rate drags it down (module 10).
A5A discrete $X$ with support $\{1,2,3\}$ has $F(1)=0.3$, $F(2)=0.7$, $F(3)=1$. $P(X=2)$ is
Point probability = jump of the CDF: $F(2)-F(2^-)=F(2)-F(1)=0.7-0.3=0.4$ (module 13).
A6Which of these is not a property of every CDF?
Discrete CDFs jump, so they are not continuous — but they are always right-continuous (module 13).
A7$X$ is continuous. Compare $P(X\le2)$ and $P(X<2)$.
$P(X=2)=0$ for continuous $X$. (For discrete $X$ they differ by the jump $P(X=2)$; $f(2)$ is a density height, not a probability — modules 13, 21.)
A8$A,B,C$ are pairwise independent. Then
The two-coin example: first coin H, same face, second coin H — pairwise independent, but $P(A\cap B\cap C)=\tfrac14\neq\tfrac18$ (module 09).
A9$X$ takes $-1,0,1$ with probability $\tfrac13$ each, and $Y=X^2$. $P(Y=1)$ is
Both $-1$ and $1$ map to 1: $\tfrac13+\tfrac13=\tfrac23$ (module 14 — merge the preimage).
A10$\mathbb V(X)=2$. Then $\mathbb V(3-2X)$ equals
Shifts don't matter, scales enter squared: $(-2)^2\cdot2=8$ (module 17).
A11$X\sim$ Binomial$(10,\,0.3)$. Its mean and variance are
$np=3$, $npq=10\cdot0.3\cdot0.7=2.1$ (module 18). "3 and 3" is the Poisson answer.
A12$X\sim$ Poisson$(2)$. $P(X=0)$ is
$e^{-\lambda}\lambda^0/0!=e^{-2}$ (module 19). $2e^{-2}$ is $P(X=1)$ (and also $P(X=2)$).
A13$f(x)=cx$ on $(0,2)$ and 0 elsewhere is a density when $c$ equals
Normalisation: $\int_0^2cx\,dx=c\cdot\tfrac{2^2}{2}=2c=1$ (module 21).
A14$X\sim$ Exponential with rate $\lambda=4$. Then $\mathbb E(X)$ and $P(X>1)$ are
$\mathbb E(X)=1/\lambda$; $P(X>x)=e^{-\lambda x}$ (module 23).
A15$X\sim N(10,25)$. $P(X<15)$ equals
The second parameter is the variance: $\sigma=5$, $z=(15-10)/5=1$ (module 24).
A16As the degrees of freedom $\nu$ grow, the Student-t distribution
$t_\nu\to N(0,1)$ as $\nu\to\infty$; small $\nu$ means heavier tails (module 25).
PART B Multiple choice — R— ⊡ ✕
R1dbinom(2, 5, 0.5) returns
d… = pmf/density at a point: $\binom52/2^5=10/32=0.3125$. p… would give $P(X\le2)=16/32=0.5$.
R2For $X\sim$ Poisson(2), which command gives $P(X>3)$?
$P(X>3)=1-P(X\le3)$ and ppois(3, 2) is $P(X\le3)$. For discrete $X$, "> 3" and "≥ 4" are the same, "> 4" is not.
R3$X\sim N(5,4)$ (variance 4). $P(X<7)$ in R is
R's normal functions take the standard deviation: $\sqrt4=2$. pnorm(7, 5, 2) $=\Phi(1)=0.8413$ (module 24 trap).
R4qnorm(0.975) returns approximately
q… = quantile (inverse CDF): the point with 97.5% of $N(0,1)$ below it, $z_{0.975}\approx1.96$ (module 25).
R5x <- c(2, 4, 6); var(x) returns
Mean 4, squared deviations 4, 0, 4, sum 8 — and var divides by $n-1=2$: 4. Dividing by $n$ would give 8/3.
R6Cards are drawn with replacement until the first ace ($p=1/13$). dgeom(2, 1/13) is
R's geometric counts failures before the first success — the slides' Geometric II: $p q^2=\tfrac1{13}(\tfrac{12}{13})^2\approx0.0655$ (module 20).
R7pexp(1, rate = 2) returns
CDF of the exponential with rate $\lambda=2$ at 1: $1-e^{-2\cdot1}$. The third option is what you get by confusing rate with mean.
R8sum(dbinom(0:10, 10, 1/6)) returns
Summing the pmf over the whole support: the normalisation rule (module 15). pbinom(10, 10, 1/6) gives the same.
R9x <- c(0, 1, 2); f <- c(0.25, 0.5, 0.25); sum(x * f) computes
$\sum x\,f(x)$ is $\mathbb E(X)$ for heads in two flips (module 16). The variance would be sum((x - 1)^2 * f) = 0.5.
R10Which command gives $P(0.2<U<0.7)$ for $U$ uniform on $(0,1)$?
$F(0.7)-F(0.2)=0.7-0.2=0.5$. dunif gives density heights (both 1, difference 0; and dunif(0.5) is 1, not a probability). punif(0.7, 0.2) is the CDF at 0.7 of a uniform on $(0.2,1)$ — the second argument is the lower end, not a second point.
R11set.seed(1); mean(runif(100000)) prints a number close to
The average of many uniform draws approaches $\mathbb E(U)=\tfrac12$ (modules 16, 22). $\tfrac1{12}$ is the variance. set.seed just makes the random draws reproducible.
R12integrate(function(x) 3*x^3, 0, 1) returns 0.75. What was computed?
$\int_0^1x\cdot3x^2\,dx=\tfrac34$ — the slides' example (module 22).
PART C Open questions — write the full solution before opening it— ⊡ ✕
O1●●modules 08–10Two suppliers
A firm buys components from supplier A (70%) and supplier B (30%). Defect rates: 2% for A, 5% for B.
(a) What is the probability that a random component is defective?
(b) A component is defective. What is the probability it came from B?
(c) A machine uses 3 components from A, independently. What is the probability that at least one is defective?
Solution
  1. (a) Total probability over the partition $\{A,B\}$: $$P(D)=P(D\mid A)P(A)+P(D\mid B)P(B)=0.02\cdot0.7+0.05\cdot0.3=0.014+0.015=0.029.$$
  2. (b) Bayes: $P(B\mid D)=\dfrac{0.05\cdot0.3}{0.029}=\dfrac{0.015}{0.029}\approx0.517$. B supplies only 30% but causes over half the defects.
  3. (c) Complement + independence: $P(\text{none defective})=0.98^3=0.941192$, so $P(\text{at least one})=1-0.941192\approx0.0588$.
O2●●modules 13, 14, 21, 22From a CDF to everything else
$F(t)=0$ for $t<0$, $F(t)=t^2/4$ for $0\le t\le2$, $F(t)=1$ for $t>2$.
(a) Check that $F$ is a CDF. (b) Find $P(X\le1)$, $P(0.5<X\le1.5)$ and $P(X=1)$. (c) Find the density $f$ and $\mathbb E(X)$. (d) Find the distribution of $Y=X^2$.
Solution
  1. (a) Values in $[0,1]$; non-decreasing ($t^2/4$ increases on $[0,2]$); $F\to0$ and $F\to1$ at the ends; continuous (at 0: $0$; at 2: $4/4=1$), hence right-continuous ✓.
  2. (b) $P(X\le1)=F(1)=\tfrac14$. $P(0.5<X\le1.5)=F(1.5)-F(0.5)=\tfrac{2.25-0.25}{4}=0.5$. $P(X=1)=0$ — no jump.
  3. (c) $f(t)=F'(t)=t/2$ on $(0,2)$, 0 elsewhere. $\mathbb E(X)=\int_0^2t\cdot\tfrac t2\,dt=\big[\tfrac{t^3}{6}\big]_0^2=\tfrac86=\tfrac43$.
  4. (d) CDF method: for $0\le y\le4$, $F_Y(y)=P(X^2\le y)=P(X\le\sqrt y)=\tfrac{(\sqrt y)^2}{4}=\tfrac y4$. So $Y$ is uniform on $(0,4)$ — a random point in $(0,4)$.
O3●modules 15–17A pmf with a constant
$X$ takes the values $0,1,2,3$ with $f(k)=c(k+1)$.
(a) Find $c$. (b) Find $P(X\ge2)$. (c) Find $\mathbb E(X)$ and $\mathbb V(X)$. (d) Find $\mathbb E(2X-1)$ and $\mathbb V(2X-1)$.
Solution
  1. (a) $c(1+2+3+4)=10c=1\Rightarrow c=\tfrac1{10}$: probabilities $0.1,0.2,0.3,0.4$.
  2. (b) $P(X\ge2)=0.3+0.4=0.7$.
  3. (c) $\mathbb E(X)=0(0.1)+1(0.2)+2(0.3)+3(0.4)=2$. $\mathbb E(X^2)=0+0.2+1.2+3.6=5$, so $\mathbb V(X)=5-2^2=1$.
  4. (d) $\mathbb E(2X-1)=2\cdot2-1=3$; $\mathbb V(2X-1)=2^2\cdot1=4$.
