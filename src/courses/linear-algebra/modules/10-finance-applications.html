FIN Portfolio return & variance in matrix form— ⊡ ✕

$n$ assets, weights $\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^n$ with $\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{w}=1$, expected returns $\boldsymbol\mu$, covariance matrix $\Sigma$ ($\Sigma_{ij}=\text{Cov}(r_i,r_j)=\rho_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j$).

$$\mu_p=\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\mu=\sum_iw_i\mu_i,\qquad\sigma_p^2=\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\mathbf{w}=\sum_i\sum_jw_iw_j\sigma_{ij}$$

Two assets, written out:

$$\sigma_p^2=\begin{pmatrix}w_1&w_2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_1^2&\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2\\\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2&\sigma_2^2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}w_1\\w_2\end{pmatrix}=w_1^2\sigma_1^2+w_2^2\sigma_2^2+2w_1w_2\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2$$

Covariance matrix properties

  • Symmetric: $\Sigma=\Sigma^{\mathsf T}$ (module 2).
  • Positive semidefinite: $\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\mathbf{w}=\text{Var}(\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{r})\ge0$ (module 8).
  • PD unless some asset is a linear combination of the others (redundant asset ⟹ $\det\Sigma=0$, singular, cannot invert).
  • Diagonal = variances; $\Sigma=D\,R\,D$ with $D=\text{diag}(\sigma_i)$ and $R$ the correlation matrix.
  • Eigen-decomposition $\Sigma=Q\Lambda Q^{\mathsf T}$: PCA; $\lambda_1/\sum\lambda_i$ = share of variance explained by the first factor (module 7).
  • Sample estimate from demeaned $T\times n$ return matrix $R$: $\hat\Sigma=\frac{1}{T-1}R^{\mathsf T}R$, rank $\le\min(T-1,n)$, so with fewer observations than assets $\hat\Sigma$ is singular.

Minimum-variance portfolio (Lagrange)

$$\min_{\mathbf{w}}\tfrac12\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\mathbf{w}\;\text{ s.t. }\;\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{w}=1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathbf{w}^*=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}},\quad\sigma^{2}_{\min}=\frac{1}{\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}}$$

Derivation: $\mathcal L=\tfrac12\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\mathbf{w}-\gamma(\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{w}-1)$; $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mathbf{w}=\Sigma\mathbf{w}-\gamma\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{0}$ ⟹ $\mathbf{w}=\gamma\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{1}$; impose the constraint to get $\gamma$. Needs $\Sigma$ invertible (module 5).

FIN OLS regression— ⊡ ✕

Model $\mathbf{y}=X\boldsymbol\beta+\boldsymbol\varepsilon$, $\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^T$, $X$ is $T\times k$ (first column of ones for the intercept).

$$\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}=(X^{\mathsf T}X)^{-1}X^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{y}$$

Replication & arbitrage (linear systems)

Payoff matrix $D$ ($m$ states × $n$ securities), prices $\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^n$, state prices $\boldsymbol\psi\in\mathbb{R}^m$:

$$D^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\psi=\mathbf{p}$$
  • Complete market ⟺ rank$(D)=m$ (every payoff reachable; $D\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ always solvable).
  • No arbitrage ⟺ exists $\boldsymbol\psi>0$ solving the system (Fundamental theorem, first part).
  • Unique $\boldsymbol\psi$ ⟺ complete and arbitrage-free. Risk-neutral probabilities $q_s=\psi_s/\sum\psi$.
EX Worked example: three-asset portfolio & a two-state replication— ⊡ ✕

Portfolio

$$\boldsymbol\mu=\begin{pmatrix}0.08\\0.12\\0.05\end{pmatrix},\;\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}0.04&0.01&0\\0.01&0.09&0.02\\0&0.02&0.01\end{pmatrix},\;\mathbf{w}=\begin{pmatrix}0.5\\0.3\\0.2\end{pmatrix}$$
  1. $\mu_p=0.5(0.08)+0.3(0.12)+0.2(0.05)=0.04+0.036+0.01=0.086$
  2. $\Sigma\mathbf{w}=\begin{pmatrix}0.02+0.003+0\\0.005+0.027+0.004\\0+0.006+0.002\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0.023\\0.036\\0.008\end{pmatrix}$
  3. $\sigma_p^2=\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf T}(\Sigma\mathbf{w})=0.0115+0.0108+0.0016=0.0239$, $\;\sigma_p\approx0.155$
  4. Is $\Sigma$ PD? $D_1=0.04>0$, $D_2=0.0036-0.0001=0.0035>0$, $D_3=0.04(0.0009-0.0004)-0.01(0.0001-0)=0.00002-0.000001=0.000019>0$ ✓

Compute $\Sigma\mathbf{w}$ first, then dot with $\mathbf{w}$: two cheap steps instead of expanding nine terms.

Replication

Two states (up/down), stock $S$: 120 / 80, bond $B$: 105 / 105. Replicate a call with strike 100: payoff 20 / 0.

$$\begin{pmatrix}120&105\\80&105\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta\\ b\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}20\\0\end{pmatrix}$$
  1. $\det=120(105)-105(80)=4200\neq0$ ⟹ market complete, unique replication.
  2. Cramer: $\Delta=\dfrac{\begin{vmatrix}20&105\\0&105\end{vmatrix}}{4200}=\dfrac{2100}{4200}=0.5$
  3. $b=\dfrac{\begin{vmatrix}120&20\\80&0\end{vmatrix}}{4200}=\dfrac{-1600}{4200}=-\dfrac{8}{21}\approx-0.381$
  4. Price today (with $S_0=100$, $B_0=100$): $0.5(100)-0.381(100)\approx11.9$

Hold half a share, borrow ≈38.1 at the risk-free rate. Same answer as binomial pricing with $q=(105-80)/(120-80)=0.625$: $(0.625\cdot20)/1.05=11.9$ ✓
