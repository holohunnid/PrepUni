Quadratic forms & definiteness

x′Ax, the shape of variance
DEF Quadratic forms— ⊡ ✕

For symmetric $A$ ($n\times n$), the quadratic form is the scalar function

$$Q(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T}A\mathbf{x}=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}a_{ij}x_ix_j$$ $$\text{2D: }\;\begin{pmatrix}x_1&x_2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_1\\x_2\end{pmatrix}=a x_1^2+2b\,x_1x_2+c\,x_2^2$$

Cross term coefficient is $2b$, so the matrix entry is half the cross-term coefficient. Any quadratic form can be written with a symmetric matrix.

$D_k$ = determinant of the top-left $k\times k$ block. $D_1=a_{11}$, $D_2=a_{11}a_{22}-a_{12}^2$, $D_n=\det A$.

Exam shortcut
2×2 symmetric: PD ⟺ $a>0$ and $\det>0$. ND ⟺ $a<0$ and $\det>0$. $\det<0$ ⟹ indefinite. $\det=0$ ⟹ semidefinite (check the sign of $a$ or the trace).

Level sets

$\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T}A\mathbf{x}=1$ is an ellipse (PD), a hyperbola (indefinite), parallel lines (PSD singular). Ellipse axes = eigenvectors, semi-axis lengths $=1/\sqrt{\lambda_i}$.

Bounds: $\lambda_{\min}\|\mathbf{x}\|^2\le\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T}A\mathbf{x}\le\lambda_{\max}\|\mathbf{x}\|^2$ (Rayleigh quotient).

INTERACTIVE Shape of x′Ax for a symmetric 2×2— ⊡ ✕

$A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{pmatrix}$. Read the classification three ways at once: sign of $Q$ on the plot, eigenvalues, and leading minors. All three must agree.

EX Worked example: classify a 3×3— ⊡ ✕
$$Q(\mathbf{x})=2x_1^2+2x_2^2+3x_3^2+2x_1x_2-2x_2x_3\quad\Rightarrow\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&0\\1&2&-1\\0&-1&3\end{pmatrix}$$

Off-diagonal entries are half the cross coefficients: $x_1x_2$ coefficient 2 ⟹ $a_{12}=1$; $x_2x_3$ coefficient −2 ⟹ $a_{23}=-1$; no $x_1x_3$ ⟹ $a_{13}=0$.

Leading principal minors

$$D_1=2>0,\qquad D_2=\begin{vmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{vmatrix}=3>0$$ $$D_3=\det A=2(6-1)-1(3-0)+0=10-3=7>0$$

All positive ⟹ positive definite.

Cross-check with trace and det

$\text{tr}=7=\sum\lambda_i>0$ and $\det=7=\prod\lambda_i>0$ are consistent with all $\lambda_i>0$ (necessary, not sufficient on their own; the minors test is the proof).

Consequence: $Q$ has a unique global minimum at $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}$; if $A$ were a covariance matrix, every non-zero portfolio would have strictly positive variance.

Trap
For semidefiniteness you must check all principal minors (every square submatrix on the diagonal), not only the leading ones. Counterexample: $\text{diag}(0,-1)$ has $D_1=0,D_2=0$ but is not PSD.
