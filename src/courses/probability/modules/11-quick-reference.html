TRAP LIST The 15 classic mistakes, collected— ⊡ ✕
  • Treating non-equally-likely outcomes as equally likely (sums of two dice).
  • Computing "at least one" directly instead of via the complement.
  • Adding $P(A)+P(B)$ for a union without subtracting the overlap.
  • Confusing $P(A\mid B)$ with $P(B\mid A)$ (base-rate fallacy).
  • Ignoring the prior in Bayes problems.
  • Confusing disjoint with independent — they are near-opposites.
  • Treating the density $f(x)$ as a probability (it can exceed 1).
  • Worrying about $\lt$ vs $\le$ for continuous RVs (no difference).
  • $E[g(X)]\ne g(E[X])$ — pushing E through a nonlinear function.
  • Adding standard deviations instead of variances.
  • Forgetting the covariance term in Var(X+Y) for dependent RVs.
  • Uncorrelated ≠ independent (except jointly normal).
  • Geometric convention mix-up: trials (mean 1/p) vs failures (mean (1−p)/p).
  • Lognormal mean: forgetting the $+\sigma^2/2$; mean ≠ median under skew.
  • Binomial approximation without the continuity correction (±½).
  • Annualising volatility with T instead of √T; adding simple returns over time.
TIP Exam checklist — before you write anything— ⊡ ✕
  • Name the experiment and the random variable. Discrete or continuous?
  • Match the story to a distribution: fixed n? waiting? rare counts? without replacement? sums of many?
  • "At least/at most" → complement or CDF difference. Union → inclusion–exclusion.
  • Normal question? Standardise, sketch the tail you want, then look up Φ.
  • Sum of RVs? Means always add; variances add only with zero covariance.
  • Sanity-check: probabilities in [0,1] and summing to 1; variance ≥ 0; answer plausible vs the 68–95–99.7 anchors.
