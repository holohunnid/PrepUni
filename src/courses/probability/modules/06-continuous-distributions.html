Continuous distributions

the zoo, part II — uniform, exponential, normal and friends
DEF Continuous uniform — total ignorance on an interval— ⊡ ✕

$X\sim U(a,b)$: every sub-interval of equal length is equally likely. The density is flat:

$$f(x)=\frac{1}{b-a}\ \ (a\le x\le b),\qquad F(x)=\frac{x-a}{b-a}$$ $$E[X]=\frac{a+b}{2},\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac{(b-a)^2}{12}$$

Probabilities are just length fractions: $P(c\le X\le d)=\frac{d-c}{b-a}$.

$U(0,1)$ is the atom of simulation: every other distribution can be manufactured from it (probability integral transform, module 8).

DEF Exponential — waiting times, memoryless— ⊡ ✕

$X\sim\text{Exp}(\lambda)$: waiting time until the next event when events arrive at rate λ (the continuous twin of the geometric; the gap between Poisson arrivals).

$$f(x)=\lambda e^{-\lambda x},\quad F(x)=1-e^{-\lambda x}\ (x\ge0),\quad E[X]=\frac1\lambda,\quad \operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac1{\lambda^2}$$

Memoryless — the defining property

$$P(X\gt s+t\mid X\gt s)=P(X\gt t)$$

Proof sketch: $\frac{P(X\gt s+t)}{P(X\gt s)}=\frac{e^{-\lambda(s+t)}}{e^{-\lambda s}}=e^{-\lambda t}$. Having already waited $s$ tells you nothing about the remaining wait. The exponential is the only continuous distribution with this property.

Min of independent exponentials: $\min(X_1,\dots,X_n)\sim\text{Exp}(\lambda_1+\dots+\lambda_n)$ — "first default among n loans" (module 8).

DEF The normal distribution — the centre of the universe— ⊡ ✕

$X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$: the bell curve, centred at $\mu$, with spread $\sigma$.

$$f(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\;e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}}$$

Why it is everywhere: sums of many small independent effects are approximately normal (Central Limit Theorem, module 9) — measurement errors, aggregate returns, sample means.

Standardisation — the only skill you need

There is no closed-form CDF; all probabilities go through the standard normal $Z\sim N(0,1)$ with CDF $\Phi$ (tabulated):

$$X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\ \Longrightarrow\ Z=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma}\sim N(0,1)$$ $$P(X\le x)=\Phi\!\Big(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\Big)$$

The recipe for every normal question: standardise → look up Φ → combine with symmetry.

Symmetry rules (tables only list z ≥ 0)

Negative z$\Phi(-z)=1-\Phi(z)$
Right tail$P(Z\gt z)=1-\Phi(z)$
Symmetric band$P(|Z|\le z)=2\Phi(z)-1$

Closure under sums — the superpower

Linear transforms and independent sums of normals are normal:

$$aX+b\sim N(a\mu+b,\;a^2\sigma^2)$$ $$X+Y\sim N(\mu_X+\mu_Y,\;\sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2)\ \ \text{(indep.)}$$

Means add; variances add (not the σ's). For correlated normals add $2\rho\sigma_X\sigma_Y$ inside the variance — module 7.

DEF Lognormal — prices, not returns— ⊡ ✕

$X$ is lognormal when its logarithm is normal: $X=e^{Y}$, $Y\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$. Positive, right-skewed — the standard model for asset prices (module 10 derives it from compounded returns).

$$E[X]=e^{\mu+\sigma^2/2},\qquad \text{median}(X)=e^{\mu}$$ $$\operatorname{Var}(X)=e^{2\mu+\sigma^2}\big(e^{\sigma^2}-1\big)$$
Trap — the σ²/2 term
$E[e^Y]\neq e^{E[Y]}$. The mean $e^{\mu+\sigma^2/2}$ is strictly above the median $e^\mu$: Jensen's inequality ($e^x$ is convex) plus right skew. Forgetting the $+\sigma^2/2$ is the single most common error in lognormal questions — and in real option-pricing formulas it is exactly the term that appears.

To compute $P(X\le x)$: take logs, standardise: $P(X\le x)=\Phi\big(\frac{\ln x-\mu}{\sigma}\big)$.

DEF Chi-square, Student t, F — the sampling trio— ⊡ ✕

Built from standard normals; they run the inference machinery of the statistics course, so meet them now:

$\chi^2_n$$Z_1^2+\dots+Z_n^2$ (indep. std normals). $E=n$, $\operatorname{Var}=2n$. Distribution of sample variances
$t_n$$\dfrac{Z}{\sqrt{\chi^2_n/n}}$. Symmetric, fatter tails than normal; → $N(0,1)$ as $n\to\infty$. $\operatorname{Var}=\frac{n}{n-2}$ for $n\gt2$. Small-sample means, and a realistic model for fat-tailed returns
$F_{m,n}$$\dfrac{\chi^2_m/m}{\chi^2_n/n}$. Ratios of variances, regression F-tests
Gamma(α,λ)generalises the exponential (α=1) and $\chi^2$ (α=n/2, λ=½); sum of α indep. Exp(λ) waits
Beta(α,β)lives on $[0,1]$, $E=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}$; models random proportions/probabilities
EX Normal computation, full exam layout— ⊡ ✕

Monthly returns $R\sim N(1\%,\ (4\%)^2)$. Find (a) P(R < 0), (b) P(−3% < R < 5%), (c) the return level exceeded with only 5% probability.

  1. (a) Standardise: $z=\frac{0-0.01}{0.04}=-0.25$. $P(R\lt 0)=\Phi(-0.25)=1-\Phi(0.25)=1-0.5987=\mathbf{0.4013}$.
  2. (b) $z$-bounds: $\frac{-0.03-0.01}{0.04}=-1$ and $\frac{0.05-0.01}{0.04}=1$. $P=\Phi(1)-\Phi(-1)=2\Phi(1)-1=2(0.8413)-1=\mathbf{0.6826}$ — the 68% band, recognised instantly since the interval is exactly $\mu\pm\sigma$.
  3. (c) Want $x$ with $P(R\gt x)=0.05$, i.e. $\Phi(z)=0.95$, so $z=1.645$. Un-standardise: $x=\mu+z\sigma=0.01+1.645(0.04)=\mathbf{7.58\%}$.
Insight
Forward questions standardise ($x\to z$), backward questions un-standardise ($z\to x=\mu+z\sigma$). Being fluent in both directions is 90% of normal-distribution marks; (c) is precisely how the VaR of module 10 is computed, with a minus sign.
