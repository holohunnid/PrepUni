Optimization Course

Finding the best point in a set — the single most-used piece of mathematics in economics and finance. Every idea arrives the same way: the question it answers, the picture, then the condition, then a worked example. The recurring theme is that first-order conditions find candidates and second-order conditions decide between them, and that convexity is what turns a local answer into a global one.

How to use: module 1 fixes the vocabulary — read it, it is short and everything later depends on the local/global distinction. Modules 2–6 do unconstrained problems, building from one variable to n; module 3 (convexity) is the conceptual centre of the course. Modules 7–9 add equality constraints and the Lagrange method, which is what the syllabus is really aiming at; module 10 is a compact extension to inequality constraints. Module 11 puts it into portfolio theory, module 12 is the night-before memory dump, and module 13 has 30 exam-style problems with full solutions.
