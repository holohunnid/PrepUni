Expected value

the probability-weighted average — and what it is not
DEF $E[X]$ — the balance point of the distribution— ⊡ ✕

$E[X]$ is the probability-weighted average of the values. Physically it is the centre of mass: the point where a beam carrying the probabilities as weights would balance.

$$E[X]=\sum_x x\,P(X=x)$$

It is not the value you expect to observe. With the distribution from module 01, $E[X]=20$ and $X$ equals 20 only half the time; in general $E[X]$ can be a value $X$ can never take at all (a fair die has $E[X]=3.5$).

Tip — symmetry shortcut
If the distribution is symmetric about a point, that point is the mean. Here the weights on 10 and 30 are equal, so $E[X]=20$ with no arithmetic. Spotting this saves a minute and removes a chance to slip.
