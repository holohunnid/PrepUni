Variance and standard deviation

dispersion, in squared units and back again
DEF Two forms of the same identity— ⊡ ✕

$V[X]$ measures dispersion: the average squared distance from the mean. Zero variance means $X$ is a constant.

$$V[X]=E\big[(X-E[X])^2\big]=\sum_x (x-\mu)^2\,P(X=x)$$ $$V[X]=E[X^2]-\big(E[X]\big)^2 \qquad \textit{(computational form)}$$

The two are the same statement. Expand $(X-\mu)^2=X^2-2\mu X+\mu^2$, take expectations term by term, and the cross term collapses to $-2\mu^2$, leaving $E[X^2]-\mu^2$.

Which form to use

SituationForm
$\mu$ is a round numberdeviation form — fewer large numbers to square
$\mu$ is ugly, or you are handed $E[X]$ and $E[X^2]$computational form
Trap
Variance is never negative. If the computational form returns a negative number you have either slipped in the arithmetic or computed $(E[X])^2$ where $E[X^2]$ was needed. Those two differ by exactly $V[X]$ — that is the whole content of the identity.

Cross-check with the computational form: $E[X^2]=25+200+225=450$, so $V[X]=450-20^2=50$. ✓

Standard deviation

$$\sigma=\sqrt{V[X]}=\sqrt{50}\approx 7.07$$

Variance lives in squared units, so it is not comparable to $X$ itself. $\sigma$ is.

Finance
$\sigma$ is volatility. A stock with $E[R]=0.10$ and $\sigma=0.20$ has $V[R]=0.04$ — quoting 0.04 as "the risk" is a unit error, not a small one: it is the difference between 4% and 20%.

Two things to notice. Drag the two outer probabilities symmetrically and the triangle does not move: symmetry pins the mean regardless of how much mass sits in the tails. But the amber $\sigma$ span keeps widening — the mean is blind to dispersion, which is exactly why the exam asks for both.

Push $x_3$ out to 65 and watch $V[X]$ grow far faster than the distance does. Variance is quadratic in the deviations: doubling how far a value sits from the mean quadruples its contribution.
