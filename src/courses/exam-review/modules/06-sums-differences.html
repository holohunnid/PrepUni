Variance of sums and differences

variances always add — only the cross term carries the sign
THM The three formulas, and the one idea behind them— ⊡ ✕
$$V[X+Y]=V[X]+V[Y]+2\operatorname{Cov}[X,Y]$$ $$V[X-Y]=V[X]+V[Y]-2\operatorname{Cov}[X,Y]$$ $$V[aX+bY]=a^2V[X]+b^2V[Y]+2ab\operatorname{Cov}[X,Y]$$

The one rule

Variances always add. Only the covariance term carries the sign of the operation. The reason is algebraic: variance is built from squares, and $(-y)^2=+y^2$. Only the cross term $2ab$ keeps the minus.

Write the deviations as $x=X-E[X]$ and $y=Y-E[Y]$. Then

$$(x-y)^2=x^2-2xy+y^2\ \longrightarrow\ V[X]+V[Y]-2\operatorname{Cov}[X,Y]$$

It is literally $(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2$ with expectations taken of both sides.

Trap
$V[X-Y]=V[X]-V[Y]$ is the most expensive error in this block, and Q4 is built to catch it. Subtracting random variables does not subtract their variances — uncertainty accumulates whichever way you combine them.

Intuition — the long-short trade

Let $X$ and $Y$ be two stock returns. $X-Y$ is a long-short position, and its variance is the volatility of the gap between them.

  • They move together ($\operatorname{Cov}>0$) → the gap barely changes → the difference is calm
  • They move oppositely ($\operatorname{Cov}<0$) → the gap swings twice as hard → the difference is wild
  • The sum behaves as the mirror image of this
Finance
This is diversification in one line. Adding assets with low or negative covariance pushes portfolio variance below the weighted average of the individual variances — and that effect lives entirely in the $2ab\operatorname{Cov}$ term.

Tip — self-check
Whenever a variance comes out as exactly 0, compute $\rho$. It must be $\pm1$, meaning one variable is an exact linear function of the other and the combination is a constant. If $\rho$ is not $\pm1$, you made an error.
INTERACTIVE The $\rho$ dial — where sum risk and difference risk go— ⊡ ✕

Sweep ρ from −1 to +1 and the two coloured bars trade places around the dashed line, always symmetrically: whatever the sum gains, the difference loses. Their total $V[X{+}Y]+V[X{-}Y]=2V[X]+2V[Y]$ is fixed — covariance moves risk between the sum and the difference, it does not create or destroy it.
