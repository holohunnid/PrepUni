Covariance and correlation

two variables, one number for how they move together
DEF Covariance — the average product of deviations— ⊡ ✕

With two variables the new question is whether they move together. Covariance is the average product of the two deviations:

$$\operatorname{Cov}[X,Y]=E\big[(X-E[X])(Y-E[Y])\big]$$ $$\operatorname{Cov}[X,Y]=E[XY]-E[X]\,E[Y] \qquad \textit{(the one you compute with)}$$

Average those products. Positive covariance means they tend to deviate in the same direction; negative, in opposite directions; zero means no linear pattern — a much weaker statement than "no pattern", as module 07 shows.

Derivation of the computational form

Write $\mu=E[X]$ and $\nu=E[Y]$ — both ordinary constants. Expand the bracket:

$$(X-\mu)(Y-\nu)=XY-\nu X-\mu Y+\mu\nu$$

Take expectations term by term, using linearity:

$$E[XY]-\nu\mu-\mu\nu+\mu\nu=E[XY]-\mu\nu$$
Tip — this one line answers Q5
Covariance is defined as the amount by which $E[XY]$ deviates from $E[X]E[Y]$. So "$\operatorname{Cov}=0$" and "$E[XY]=E[X]E[Y]$" are the same sentence written twice. No independence appears anywhere in the derivation.

Correlation — covariance made unit-free

$$\rho=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}[X,Y]}{\sigma_X\,\sigma_Y},\qquad -1\le\rho\le 1$$

Covariance depends on the units of both variables, so its magnitude means nothing on its own — rescale $X$ from euros to cents and the covariance multiplies by 100. Dividing by the two standard deviations puts it on a fixed $[-1,1]$ scale. $\rho=\pm1$ means the two variables lie exactly on a straight line.

TIP Special cases worth memorising— ⊡ ✕
$\operatorname{Cov}[X,X]=V[X]$a variable covaries with itself by exactly its own variance — variance is the diagonal of the covariance matrix
$\operatorname{Cov}[X,Y]=\operatorname{Cov}[Y,X]$symmetric — the definition is a product, and products commute
$\operatorname{Cov}[a+bX,\ c+dY]=bd\,\operatorname{Cov}[X,Y]$constants drop out (they have no deviations), slopes multiply — the two-variable version of $V[a+bX]=b^2V[X]$
$\operatorname{Cov}[X,c]=0$a constant never deviates, so there is nothing to co-move with
